H32: does an egoist act like a utilitarian under free exit and amplification?

Exact dynamic programming, 3005 solves, 241.6 s, $0. Agents feel nothing; "pleasure" is a gloss for a modeled hedonic state. Every number is assumed. Prereg docs/results/h32-prereg.md; results docs/results/h32.md.

Interpretation

With all three mechanisms off the egoist and the utilitarian choose disjoint weeks (D 1.00), and the egoist's policy loses 23% of what the pair could have. The control works.

Free exit on its own does not make the egoist attend to the partner. At the defaults the partner leaves after two weeks, the egoist lives alone, and the pleasure gap shrinks to 8% only because living apart is better for the partner than being neglected. The policies stay disjoint (D 1.00). Where the outside option is 0.4 or more, the utilitarian drives the partner out too, and the two 'agree' only because both end the bond.

Amplification is the strongest single lever once it is strong enough. In this menu, from alpha 1.5 the shared activities both people like become the egoist's own best weeks, and the policies match with no exit and no giving. At the default alpha 1 it moves the egoist halfway (D 0.50). Amplification plus giving closes the whole gap at every alpha of 0.5 or more.

At the defaults giving closes the most of the pleasure gap (Shapley 0.105 of 0.231), then amplification (0.076), then exit (0.049). On policy distance exit closes nothing at the defaults.

Free exit made the egoist converge in exactly one of 30 cells (alpha 1, outside option 0.8), where amplification alone did not. That cell disappears when the partner's exit is a little noisy (5 percent) or a quarter blind to its own pleasure. H32's strict pattern held in 0 cells, because without amplification the utilitarian also chooses to split there. Exit can also widen the gap: at alpha 0.5 it lets the egoist shed the partner.

7 of 11 predictions held. H32 as stated: not supported (its pattern holds in 0 of 30 grid cells).
