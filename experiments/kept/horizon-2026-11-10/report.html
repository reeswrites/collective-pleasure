Lifetime pleasure versus greedy (horizon study)

What this study asked

You said lifetime pleasure is the right measure, and that caring about the whole future should mean you plan instead of grabbing the best thing now. The question: is grabbing the most pleasure right now ever worse than planning for a whole life? This study builds a small model and solves it exactly, so the answer is a computation, not a guess.

A small household decision problem solved exactly (8912 reachable states, 7 actions). Every parameter is assumed; see docs/results/horizon.md. Agents feel nothing; pleasure is a gloss for a modeled hedonic state.

With all carried-over state switched off, greedy loses exactly 0 over 56 grid cells. With it on, greedy loses up to 24.5% of lifetime pleasure at gamma 1 over 26 weeks.

Greedy loses nothing at the far left, about 3.2% at 0.9, 15.5% at 0.95, and 24.5% over 26 weeks when the future counts fully. Greedy first loses at a discount of 0.38. The more you care about your whole life, the more planning matters.

The proof in plain words

The lemma: grabbing the best thing now is guaranteed to be the best plan only if nothing you do today changes tomorrow. If a choice leaves any trace that later pleasure can see, some situation exists where greedy loses.

A computer checked one case. You can have the concert now, or a solo treat now and the concert next week. Concert twice in a row totals 25.35. Treat then concert totals 26.40.

Greedy cannot see this because it looks one step ahead. Waiting a week makes the concert worth more, and that anticipation pays off later, where greedy never looks. Discounting the future does not fix it. It only changes how much each later week counts.

Your questions, answered by this model

The concert

Does a concert steal from future pleasures? It depends on how often you go. Going every week is a net loss of 5.6 pleasure units over 26 weeks.

Going every 3 to 4 weeks is a net gain, about +1.1 at 3 weeks and +2.2 at 4. The boundary is about 3 weeks.

The costs are real but small. The benefits are anticipation, a vivid memory and a bond. Spaced out, the benefits win.

Seeing someone

The best gap between meetings grows with how deep the friendship is: 19 days for a quick-fading bond, 139 days for a deep one (half-lives of 10 and 90 days).

The old remembered-self score says meet every 6 to 13 days. It counts the big reunion and ignores the days apart, so it always wants more meetings.

If two friends have different bonds, one gap cannot suit both. At 139 days the faster-fading friend gets 56% less value than at their own best gap (42 days). That is the slow fade.

How much memory counts is the biggest lever. Tripling it moves the best gap for the middle friendship from 86 to 108 days.

The one-self view

You said the experiencing self is the remembering self. So each moment counts what you feel now, plus vivid memories you re-live, plus anticipation.

A good reunion worth 15 in the moment is worth 24.0 once remembered. A betrayal costing 20 keeps costing, 51.3 in total. Long mild stretches fade from memory fast.

The two old scores are special cases of this one. Switch memory off and you get the experienced score. Switch the present off and you get the remembered score.

What surprised us

Simple rules of thumb did worse than greedy. At gamma 0.9, greedy reaches 97% of the best total, weekly ritual 74%, rotating every week 61%. Planning is not the same as rotating.

A long commitment only pays when the future counts at least about 0.903. We predicted it would pay at 0.9, and it did not (prediction P4 missed).

Loss does not always grow with the horizon either. At gamma 0.9 it peaks near 13% and falls back to 3.2% by 26 weeks (prediction P2 missed).

How much to trust this

This is a toy model. The numbers are assumed, chosen to stand for your ideas, and they are not measured from real people.

It proves the logic and the shape of the answer. It does not give facts about how any real life goes.

The philosophical choices (lifetime as the measure, one self, how memory counts) are stated in ADR 0017, where you can change them.

Why it matters for AI alignment

An AI that optimizes the next reply or the next step is a greedy agent. It can drain things its reward does not show, such as trust or the other person's patience. That is reward hacking.

Here is a small exact case of when that costs and how much: up to 25% of lifetime pleasure, and it starts once the future counts at all (0.38 and up).
