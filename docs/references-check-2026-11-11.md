# Citation check (2026-11-11)

Nothing here is a result. This page checks every literature citation in the repo that was marked "from memory", "verify" or "unverified". It does not edit those docs. The coordinator applies the corrections.

Method: web search against publisher, PubMed, PMC, PhilArchive, RePEc and library records. Abstracts and catalogue records only; no full texts were read. "Searched" means the bibliographic data and the abstract-level claim were seen this session. "Recalled" marks a detail I could not re-fetch. No quotes over 15 words.

## Summary

- Citations checked: 26 (rows 1-27 below; row 18 is a pointer row and is not counted).
- CONFIRMED: 21. CORRECTED: 3. WEAKER THAN STATED: 2. NOT FOUND: 0. WRONG: 0.
- Names on the request list that appear nowhere in the repo (so nothing to check): Aron, Becker, Rozin, Wilson et al., Lucas and Diener, McEwen, Gable, Noe and Hammerstein, a second Sinhababu source beyond theory.md. See the last section.

Corrections that change an argument (not just a detail):

1. **Hirschman (H32c, h32b-prereg.md).** The preregistered interaction "voice matters more when exit is backed by a real alternative" is only half of Hirschman. His headline claim is the opposite sign for voice use: easy exit tends to crowd voice out. The other half is that a credible exit threat makes voice more effective per use. The prereg must say which quantity it predicts (voice use or voice effect), or the result will be read against the wrong baseline.
2. **Kahneman, experienced vs remembered self (horizon.md section on "Which self").** The 1993 paper does not set up that distinction. The instant/remembered-utility framework is Kahneman, Wakker and Sarin 1997. Fixing the cite is a detail, but ADR 0017's framing of "two named special cases" should cite the 1997 paper.
3. **Zajonc, then Berlyne (synthesis Q12, "liking curve rises, then falls").** Zajonc 1968 supports only the rise (exposure enhances attitude). The fall and the role of complexity come from Berlyne's two-factor model and later exposure work. Listing both as support for one curve credits the fall to the wrong source.
