# Hedonic technology: the author's notes as design implications (2026-11-10)

Wording rule: agents feel nothing. "Pleasure" and "suffering" are glosses for a modeled hedonic state (ADR 0010).

Status: a reading of the author's private notes. Nothing here is a result. Every prediction below is a candidate to preregister, not a finding. Sources were read in full from Notion and exo, read-only. Two exo notes could not be retrieved: "human flourishing is just the right delivery schedule for a unique..." and "pleasure as the detector of value" both came back as other notes (the exo tool returns the nearest match, and the first returned an empty body). Claims about those two are not used below. If the author wants them used, paste the text.

Note dates matter. "Relationships as a Hedonic Technology" was edited 2026-11-10 and is the newest and most important. "Commitment Is a Pleasure Technology" is a draft skeleton from 2026-09-02 that says its own argument is "in the joins".

## 1. The author's theory in their own terms

**Relationships as a Hedonic Technology** (Notion, edited 2026-11-10).
- Relationships are a reliable way to create pleasure for both parties. Norms are the shape of the relationship: its boundaries and containers.
- Quote: "You and the people around you are active participants in each other's pleasure."
- Pleasure is not the only thing a relationship gives. Leading with pleasure makes many other things fall out.
- Relationships are not about extracting from the other. If one person's pleasure comes at the other's explicit suffering, reconsider the arrangement. Both should be better off and free to leave.
- Relationships are also not about getting only good things. Sustaining one costs something. The give and take should leave both better off in the long run.
- Ways to feel pleasure with others fall into kinds: joint attention (a third thing both enjoy), loops (each person's output is the other's input: games, improv, sex, co-creation), and unidirectional dynamics (giver and receiver, performer and witness). Attention underlies all of them.
- Some pleasures are solitary (reading). Parallel play is a different thing from joint attention. A book club creates the pleasure of discussing, not of reading.
- People differ in style: give, share, receive. The author's own self-assessment: likes to give and share, finds receiving nice but lacks initiators, and needs an initiator with strong visions that overlap theirs.
- Group size changes what is possible: dyads, triads (mediator or connector), bands and teams, crowds with anonymity and shared purpose.
- Be intentional. Spend time with people you like. Invest in shared pleasure. Cut anything in a relationship you do not want, and keep only what you want or must have to keep it going. Pleasure makes you want to keep the friend. The must-haves make it possible to maintain.
- Compatibility beats attraction. Attraction is easier to find than compatibility, so put yourself in compatible groups and hope attraction comes.
- Some partners cannot take your pleasure seriously, which makes a positive hedonic relationship hard. If you share a surplus you have a duty of sorts to share. Bear the cost of friends without complaint.
- Unresolved in the note: how much we owe each other, between "you don't owe anyone your time" and "inconvenience is the cost of community".

**Commitment Is a Pleasure Technology** (Notion, draft, 2026-09-02).
- Quote: "Commitment is the instrument that converts present friction into recalled pleasure."
- Without commitment you can only collect pleasures that resolve inside a day. Total pleasure is counted over a life, and long projects (goals, games, relationships) give the payoff structure. Having the goal matters more than reaching it.
- Commitment is Type 2 fun: unpleasant now, pleasant in recollection. Being changed by a partner is friction.
- Design flaw: a self-authored commitment can be re-authored exactly when it starts to cost something. Two repairs: commit to timescales ("I am not evaluating this for a year") and to dimensions (social, space, epistemic, linguistic, time, economic, narrative, aesthetic), not to activities.
- The author's aversion may be to enforcement (guilt, scorekeeping, manufactured debt) and not to reliance. The author marks this split as untested.
- The 2024 "shared goals" checklist smuggled in duty. The author says this should be made visible.

**Optimal Stimulation and Hedonic Homeostasis** (Notion, 2026-09-19).
- Quote: "too much or too little stimulation is heavily felt and penalized by our nervous systems."
- Hypoarousal and hyperarousal both hurt. Optimal level is relative to the person (baseline arousal differs) and to the moment (fatigue shifts it). Arousal is scheduled like insulin response: up, down, or hold.
- Three maintenance zones: flow (high), cruising (mid), rest (low). Flow cannot be the goal of every hour.
- Sensory-specific satiety and receptor downregulation: the same stimulus repeated loses value. Fixes: micro-novelty at low switching cost, passive breaks, scarcity, full attention.
- Wanting (prediction error, dopamine) and liking (opioid) adapt by different mechanisms. Overstimulating wanting burns out liking. Rules of thumb recorded: reward success near 50 to 70 percent, stop at about 80 percent, space repeats by many times the half-life. These come from an AI-generated passage inside the note, not from the author's own claim.
- Modern environments are hyperstimulating, so people carve out quiet spaces. This atomizes people and keeps them apart.

**unbundling love** (Notion, 2026-11-03).
- "Love" is the culture's name for a bundle: care, affection, intimacy, desire, commitment, logistics. The bundle usually sits on one partner and family.
- Unbundle so a life holds more closeness than one partner can supply. Move in order: care first (build a floor, because losing care is the real danger), then affection and intimacy, then logistics and commitment, desire last.
- Quote: "Bundle thickly, never solely." Unbundled lives cost coordination, so keep a core. Do not force strands apart if they work together for you.
- Tool: a grid of strands by people, marking who holds what.

**Engineering On-Demand Pleasure Technology** (Notion, 2026-09-25).
- Pleasure technology has evolved with addiction technology. The aim is safe, not extractive, delivery. Harm reduction: hits of pleasure without comedown.
- Anticipation can be designed without restricting access: scheduling, preparation, bigger units, synchronizing with other people, restoring appetite (make the person cold before the sauna), and deliberate deferral with a ritual.
- Quote: "Put it at the trigger, before commitment, never inside the consumption." Willpower friction and price friction fail. Structural or social friction works, because something outside you holds the time.
- Metrics list: find, supply, serve, end-to-end latency, dose size and repeat interval, closure rate, next-day verdict. Only the last two measure liking, and almost nobody tracks them. A fast system can drift into the trap without any metric noticing.
- Novelty should rotate. Experiences are needed as material even if drugs exist. Calm technologies (warmth, rocking) signal safety. Intense ones (spice, cold) give a small safe threat.

**Hedonic Totalitarianism** (Notion, stub, 2026-09-13).
- Only a title, "Brave New World", "Love Drugs", and a saved liberal quote: people pursue different, revisable conceptions of the good, and a liberal state governs by general rules. I read this as a worry about pleasure engineering imposed from above. The note does not say so. It is a stub.

**Hedonism For Dummies** (Notion, 2026-09-21).
- Steps: survive, know (what do I like, how does my nervous system differ), do (explore and exploit), amplify (discernment, presence), systematize (make joy near frictionless: keep stock, menus, queues, trusted curators, track what you tried and why).
- Long form adds create, consume, cavort, commune, taste mapping, taste expansion, and systems development. Preferences are partly changeable, while sensory inputs are fixed.

**exo: sensory hacking and the good** (2026-07-25). Pleasure splits into wanting (the chase, dopamine) and liking (the capture, opioid). Art and sensory design manipulate this split. A true good needs more than the chase.

**exo: Simple Pleasures** (2023). A list of small, particular things: green furniture, trees in wind, secondhand bookstores, an album that sustains the hype. Pleasure here is specific and idiosyncratic.

## 2. Where the current simulation agrees and where it misses or contradicts

What the simulation has (ADR 0010, docs/results/pleasure-demo.md): four needs on real clocks (rest, connection, money, security), aspirations from persona weights, one pleasure channel plus offsettable and non-offsettable suffering, spiral positions, trust assumed, negotiated commitments to stay, a shared pot, and the freedom and cooperation scores. Latest result: 89 percent of pairings upward, relational share of pleasure 0.47 but produced by persona weights, rules help only against pushy personas, trust never moved, 212 of 328 agreed households made no commitment.

### Agreements

- **Pleasure is the good, and it is personal.** Persona weights and need salience encode "you are different, what works for others won't work for you" (Hedonism For Dummies, Simple Pleasures). Good fit.
- **Suffering that cannot be netted.** The author says a partner's pleasure must not come at their explicit suffering. The non-offsettable channel does this. Good fit.
- **Both must be better off and free to leave.** The leave rule and exit terms match.
- **Compatibility over attraction.** The pairing grid shows well-matched hand personas do fine and mismatches fail. This matches "put yourself in compatible groups".
- **Needs have real clocks.** Matches the homeostatic framing in a coarse way: a need decays and must be met.

### Misses and contradictions

1. **No hedonic adaptation.** Time together yields the same pleasure in week 150 as in week 2. The author's theory (sensory-specific satiety, tolerance, wanting adaptation) says repeated identical stimuli lose value and variety or spacing restores it. The model has no diminishing return on repeated activity, so the best policy is "do the same best thing every week". This is the largest miss, and it also explains why the demo has nothing to say about novelty (new connections add 0.002).
2. **No optimal stimulation level.** Fatigue is a cost, not an arousal state. The author says too little and too much both hurt, that optimum differs by person and by moment, and that flow, cruising and rest are three valid zones. The model has no under-stimulation penalty for a quiet household and no over-stimulation penalty for a busy one. Rest and connection are monotone needs.
3. **Wanting and liking are one number.** The notes separate chase from capture and say overstimulating wanting burns out liking. The model has one pleasure value. (ADR 0010 lists wanting and liking under version 3 backlog, so this is a known gap.)
4. **No delivery schedule or anticipation.** The author's engineering note says anticipation is made by scheduling, preparation, bigger units and synchronization, and that a queue without a ritual is a debt. In the model every action pays off the week it is taken. A planned Saturday dinner earns nothing before Saturday. Time-together sessions (175 per stable household) are unscheduled draws.
5. **Commitment is modeled as a constraint, not as a pleasure technology.** The author claims commitment converts present friction into lifetime pleasure by enabling long projects. The model treats a commitment as a bind that costs a breach penalty. A kept commitment gives trust, but trust never changed. The result report says commitments "change almost nothing the model measures". The author's theory predicts the opposite, so the model is probably missing the mechanism, not disproving the theory.
6. **Lifetime accounting is absent.** The model reports mean weekly pleasure and a final spiral class. The author says pleasure is counted over a whole life and that some things (Type 2 fun) are only pleasant in recollection. There is no recall term and no long-project payoff.
7. **No friction that costs now and pays later.** Offsettable suffering is netted against pleasure each week. The author's category of costs that are chosen and later converted (being changed by a partner, giving) has no place. The two suffering types do not include "chosen cost".
8. **No unbundling.** The household world is a dyad plus Friend C. The author's central structural claim is that closeness should flow through many bonds, each carrying some strands, with a thick core. The model has one partner who carries every strand (care, intimacy, logistics, commitment). Friends only add a friends term.
9. **No give, share, receive styles.** Pro-sociality is a scalar from the giving axis and togetherness weight. The author distinguishes roles (giver, receiver, performer, witness, initiator) and notes that a giver needs an initiator who plans. Persona styles cannot mismatch in this way.
10. **No joint attention, loop, or unidirectional pleasure.** Time together is one pooled action. The author lists three kinds of relational pleasure with different requirements. Only some need both people to enjoy the same thing.
11. **Giving is not pleasure in the giver.** The author likes giving and sharing as a pleasure in itself. In the model a gift is a cost to the giver (the gift rule moved suffering onto the giver in earlier runs).
12. **Rules protect and do not produce.** This is the gap the author's condition names. Rules are walls and prices. The author's "technology" frame is about structures that generate pleasure: rituals, scheduled shared time, norms that make a partner's initiative easy. The rule vocabulary (ADICO cards) has no card whose effect is to create a pleasure opportunity.
13. **Trust is a bank balance with no events.** The author does not mention trust directly, but says expectation and guilt cause shutdown and that reliance can be fine without enforcement. The model's trust never falls because breaches never happen. It cannot represent reliance without enforcement.
14. **Hedonic totalitarianism is untouched.** The stub worries about imposed pleasure. The model scores rule sets by pleasure and reports freedom beside it, which is the right guard, but there is no scenario where a rule set raises measured pleasure by overriding a persona's own revisable taste.

## 3. Concrete model changes the notes imply

Each item gives mechanism, what it adds, source note, and a falsifiable prediction. Predictions are phrased so that a null is informative. All depend on assumed parameters, and the effect size should be reported with its sensitivity to them. These should go through the ADR 0015 process (prereg first, power check, Holm).

### M1. Hedonic adaptation with recovery (satiety)
- **Mechanism.** Each repeatable pleasure source (time together, an activity type, a friend contact) carries a habituation level h in [0,1]. Pleasure from the source is scaled by (1 - h). h rises with each repeat and decays with time away. Variety (a different activity within the same category) resets part of h at a small cost. This is the low-tax micro-novelty idea.
- **Adds.** A per-source state in `hedonic.py`, a `variety` choice inside the time-together action, and one adaptation block in `HedonicConfig`.
- **Source.** Optimal Stimulation and Hedonic Homeostasis; Engineering On-Demand Pleasure Technology (spacing, scarcity, 80 percent stop).
- **Prediction P1.** With adaptation on, a persona policy that uses the same best action every week gets lower mean pleasure over 156 weeks than a policy that rotates and spaces, in at least 70 percent of pairings. With adaptation off (current model) the same-action policy wins in most pairings. A falsifier: rotation fails to beat repetition when adaptation is on, which would mean the rotation cost assumption dominates.

### M2. Optimal stimulation curve with a personal baseline
- **Mechanism.** Each agent has an arousal state a that moves with activity load (social events, work, noise) and returns to a personal baseline. Pleasure from an activity is an inverted U in the gap between a and the agent's optimum for that activity type (flow, cruising, rest zones). Under-stimulation and over-stimulation both add suffering. An agent can also schedule compensatory activities (up after a quiet period, down after a busy one).
- **Adds.** An arousal variable, a per-persona optimum (higher for sensation seekers, lower for sensitive types), and a rest-or-lull action as a first-class choice.
- **Source.** Optimal Stimulation and Hedonic Homeostasis.
- **Prediction P2.** Pleasure as a function of weekly social load follows an inverted U for every persona, with the peak lower for high-sensitivity personas. In pairs where the two optima differ, the best household schedule gives the sensitive partner more rest weeks and the seeker more outside contact than the even split does, and a rule forcing equal togetherness lowers the sensitive partner's pleasure relative to a rule that lets each set their own dose. Falsifier: the equal-togetherness rule does no worse.

### M3. Scheduled events with anticipation and preparation
- **Mechanism.** A plan action commits a future slot (a dinner, a film, a call). The planned event pays an anticipation term in the weeks before (growing with lead time up to a cap) and then a larger payoff on the day, with the payoff reduced if the slot is cancelled. Preparation work (cooking, hosting) is a cost that raises the payoff and adds to anticipation. A queue that holds more than a few items without ritual slots adds a small debt term.
- **Adds.** A schedule list per household, plan and cancel actions, an anticipation term in the pleasure sum, and a rule card that creates a standing slot (a weekly ritual, a movie night).
- **Source.** Engineering On-Demand Pleasure Technology (anticipation without restriction, synchronizing with other people); Relationships as a Hedonic Technology (rituals).
- **Prediction P3.** Households under a rule that creates a weekly ritual slot (both agree to a fixed time together) have higher mean pleasure than households with the same total time together taken ad hoc, with the gap larger when adaptation (M1) is on. Falsifier: equal pleasure at equal dose. That would mean the benefit of rituals is only in the dose and not in the anticipation, which is a real answer.

### M4. Commitment as a converter of present friction into later pleasure
- **Mechanism.** Add a chosen-cost channel: some actions (honoring a commitment, being changed by a request, a hard conversation) cost now and are tagged as chosen. A kept commitment starts a long-project accumulator that pays out slowly: it unlocks higher-ceiling pleasures (shared long projects, deeper connection) that need a minimum duration, and it adds a recall term that credits earlier friction after a delay. Break the commitment and the accumulator resets and trust falls. Also split reliance (a partner organizes their week around you, no cost to the other) from enforcement (guilt, scorekeeping), and let an agent that dislikes enforcement still accept reliance.
- **Adds.** A chosen-cost suffering type (not netted as non-offsettable, but counted in the weekly cost), a long-project accumulator, a recall term, commitment cards by timescale (not behavior), and an enforcement-versus-reliance split in the rule vocabulary and in persona traits.
- **Source.** Commitment Is a Pleasure Technology.
- **Prediction P4 (the author's central claim).** In households that make a timescale commitment, mean weekly pleasure over the first 26 weeks is lower than in matched households without one (friction up front), and mean pleasure over weeks 52 to 156 and cumulative lifetime pleasure are higher, in at least 60 percent of pairings. Falsifier: the crossover does not occur, or occurs only when the long-project payoff parameter is set above a stated bound (report the break-even value). Also: commitments to timescales survive longer than commitments to behaviors under the same cost shocks, since the former does not get re-authored.
- **Sub-prediction P4b.** Personas with high enforcement aversion but low reliance aversion do better under a reliance-only commitment than under an enforced one. Falsifier: no difference.

### M5. Unbundled relationship networks versus one partner carrying everything
- **Mechanism.** Replace the single connection need with strand needs (care, affection, intimacy, desire, commitment, logistics) that any bond can serve. Each bond holds a subset of strands. A household can run a thick core (partner plus housemates) and several thin bonds (an ideas friend, a history friend). Maintenance of each bond costs time and attention (a coordination cost, convex in the number of bonds). Add a public floor for care so that losing a bond never drops care to zero, as a toggle.
- **Adds.** A bond list per agent with strand sets, per-bond maintenance cost, a strand-by-people grid as a diagnostic output, and rule cards that assign strands to bonds. The household world needs 2 to 4 additional friend agents instead of one passive Friend C.
- **Source.** unbundling love; Relationships as a Hedonic Technology (groups by size, triads, dyads).
- **Prediction P5.** In a network with at least three bonds each holding at least two strands (thick core plus thin bonds), mean pleasure is higher and the share of partner-weeks in non-offsettable suffering is lower than in a one-partner-carries-all network with the same total time budget, up to a coordination-cost threshold. Beyond about five bonds, pleasure falls (the cautions "keep a core", "bundle thickly, never solely"). Under shocks to one bond (rupture, illness, a move), the unbundled network loses less pleasure. Falsifier: pleasure is monotone in the number of bonds (no coordination ceiling), or the one-partner network wins under shocks. Both are informative.
- **Sub-prediction P5b.** Ordering matters: unbundling care first (public floor present) beats unbundling commitment first, measured by non-offsettable suffering after a shock. Falsifier: order has no effect.

### M6. Give, share, receive styles and role matching
- **Mechanism.** Each persona gets a style vector over give, share, receive, plus an initiator trait. Pleasure from time together depends on the type of activity: joint attention needs overlapping interests, loops need two active partners, unidirectional needs a giver and a receiver with a witness who enjoys witnessing. Giving yields pleasure to the giver in proportion to the receiver's response, and a mismatched pair (two receivers with no initiator) stalls.
- **Adds.** Style fields on personas, activity types with role requirements, and a pleasure term for the giver.
- **Source.** Relationships as a Hedonic Technology.
- **Prediction P6.** Pairs with complementary roles (a giver and a receiver, or two sharers with overlapping interests) have higher time-together pleasure than same-role pairs at equal time, and a rule card that assigns an initiator role (one person plans, one person attends) raises pleasure for two-receiver pairs. Falsifier: role complementarity explains less than 10 percent of the variance in pair pleasure beyond the existing weights.

### M7. Wanting versus liking, with spacing
- **Mechanism.** Split pleasure into a wanting component (drives action choice, adapts to predictability) and a liking component (the experienced payoff, adapts by tolerance with a slower clock). Overuse of one source raises wanting and lowers liking, giving "chase without payoff". This is the version 3 backlog item in the goal file.
- **Adds.** Two values per source, a policy that chooses by wanting but is scored by liking, and a closure-rate metric (share of sessions that end at a natural stop).
- **Source.** Engineering On-Demand Pleasure Technology (metrics list); exo "sensory hacking and the good"; Optimal Stimulation note.
- **Prediction P7.** A policy that maximizes wanting (do whatever is most attractive now) ends with a higher dose and a lower next-week-verdict than a spaced policy, and a rule card that sets a spacing norm raises mean liking but lowers wanting-driven activity. Falsifier: wanting-maximizing does as well on liking.

### M8. Hedonic technology cards: rules that produce, not only prevent
- **Mechanism.** Add a rule class whose effect is to create a pleasure opportunity: a ritual slot (M3), a role assignment (M6), a variety budget (M1), a rest window (M2), a renewal date for a commitment (M4), a strand assignment (M5). Score them separately from protective cards, and report "pleasure produced by rule" as the gap between a household with the card and the matched one without it, with persona weights held fixed.
- **Adds.** A producing-versus-protecting tag on cards, and an attribution report that answers the author's condition directly: how much of the relational pleasure traces to a rule and not to persona weights.
- **Source.** Relationships as a Hedonic Technology (norms are the shape of relationships); Engineering On-Demand Pleasure Technology.
- **Prediction P8.** After M1 to M5 exist, at least one producing card raises mean pleasure by more than 0.03 (the current rule effect) for well-matched hand personas, where the imposed baseline currently wins by 0.028. Falsifier: producing cards still do nothing for matched personas. That would show the technology frame adds nothing in this world, which is a result.

### M9. Metrics from the author's own list
- **Mechanism.** Report closure rate, next-day verdict (a lagged pleasure check), dose size and repeat interval, and end-to-end latency (time from want to pleasure) as household-level outputs beside pleasure, freedom, and cooperation. The author says speed and throughput metrics will drift toward the trap without the liking metrics.
- **Source.** Engineering On-Demand Pleasure Technology.
- **Prediction P9.** A rule set that maximizes throughput (pleasure events per week) scores worse on closure rate and next-day verdict than a spaced set, in a model with M1 and M7. Falsifier: no divergence, meaning the trap quadrant does not exist in this world.

### Suggested order

M1 and M3 first (small, local, and the cheapest test of whether rules can produce anything). Then M4 (the author's central claim and the biggest gap: commitments barely mattered). Then M5 (needs the world to grow). M2, M6, M7 are independent and can follow. Run M8 and M9 as reporting layers as each lands. Keep each slice shippable on its own and keep the old runs reproducible.

## 4. Tensions and open questions for the author

1. In "Commitment Is a Pleasure Technology" you say pleasure counts over the whole life, and in an earlier note you said the future is unknowable so you can only commit to now. You wrote that the presentism breaks. Should the simulation use a lifetime horizon (and a recall term), or a present-weighted one? Which would you want a persona to have?
2. Is your aversion to enforcement only, so that a partner relying on you without guilt or scorekeeping would be fine? You flagged this as untested. If it is true, should the model let a persona accept reliance but refuse any penalty card?
3. You say commitment is Type 2 fun, pleasant in recollection. Should friction that was chosen count as suffering at all while it happens, or only as a cost that the recall term later credits?
4. You say relationships should not be transactional, and also that the give and take should leave both better off in the long run. Over what period, and who checks? Is a lopsided stretch (one partner giving more for months) a failure, or normal?
5. How much do we owe each other? You name both slogans, "you don't owe anyone" and "inconvenience is the cost of community", and say it gets tricky. In the model, what should the default owed amount be beyond negotiated terms: zero (your seed), or a floor of decency?
6. Unbundling: your note says to keep a core and bundle thickly, never solely. How thick, and how many bonds? In the model, should the core be required, or is a life with no core a valid persona?
7. You say attraction is easier than compatibility and to join compatible groups. Should the model let agents choose who to pair with (and which group), instead of testing every fixed pairing? That would change what rules are for.
8. You describe pleasures that need no shared interest (a giver and a witness). Does the receiver's pleasure need to be real for the giver's pleasure to count, or can the giver enjoy giving into a void? This sets whether giving is a pleasure term or only a cost.
9. Is pleasure the only measure, or only the lead? You say that leading with pleasure makes many things fall out, and also that it is not the only thing relationships give (stability, ease). Should security and money stay in the pleasure score, as they now are, which makes the model a poverty-and-security measure in most runs?
10. Hedonic totalitarianism: you saved a note on liberal rules over revisable conceptions of the good but left it a stub. If a rule set raises measured pleasure by shaping tastes (norms that teach a persona to like something), is that a win, or a violation? The model currently has no taste change, so it cannot even ask.
11. Optimal stimulation: the note records rules of thumb (50 to 70 percent reward, stop at 80 percent, spacing at many times the half-life) that came from an AI passage. Do you endorse them as parameters, or should adaptation rates stay free and be swept?
12. Engineering note: you separate calm technologies (safety signals) from intense ones (small safe threat). Do relationships have both, and should the model give a household two modes, safe and spicy, with different arousal effects?
13. Two notes ("delivery schedule" and "pleasure as the detector of value") could not be fetched. Is "right delivery schedule for a unique..." the thesis that M1 to M3 should be built around, and does "pleasure as detector of value" mean that rules should be judged by whether pleasure tracks something real, not only high pleasure?
