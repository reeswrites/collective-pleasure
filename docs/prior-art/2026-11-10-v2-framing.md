# Prior-art check: the v2 framing (2026-11-10)

Nothing here is a result. This checks whether the new framing in docs/goal.md and ADRs 0007 and 0011 has been done before. It is a quick scan, not a literature review.

## Read depth, stated honestly

- Search engine snippets and summaries for every query. No search hit was read in full.
- Abstract pages fetched (abstract only, no full text): LegalSim, SocioHack, Multi-Agent Empowerment, Ellis et al. (Empower), Choose Your Agent, Jaidka and Ahmed, Belardinelli et al. (bisimulation), Ye and Steinhardt (norm enforcement).
- Everything marked "recalled" comes from my own knowledge and was not fetched this session.
- Absence of a hit is weak evidence. A "none" below means "not found in about 15 queries", not "does not exist".
- Several hits are 2026 preprints. Some have been revised since. Check versions before citing.

## 1. Capability level at which LLM agents exploit outside a formal model

Our claim: proofs hold inside the model. Agents above some capability level find seams the model checker did not predict. Measure that level and its price.

| Work | Year | What it does | Overlap |
|---|---|---|---|
| LegalSim: multi-agent simulation of legal systems for discovering procedural exploits. arXiv:2510.03405 https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.03405 | 2025 | Agents act under a machine-readable rules engine. Ranks PPO, bandit, LLM and heuristic policies on exploit finding. Exploits are valid inside the rules and harmful to the system. | Partial. Same spirit (find loopholes in codified rules). The exploits are inside the formal action space. No capability threshold. |
| Liu, Mou, Yan, Wei, He. "Large language models hack rewards, and society" (SocioHack). arXiv:2606.04075 https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04075 | 2026 | 72 societal sandboxes. RL-trained models find regulatory loopholes. Direct "find loopholes" requests are refused above 65%. RL-found loopholes trigger near-zero refusals. | Partial. Closest on "rules gamed by learned agents". No formal model or checker comparison seen. No scaling finding in the abstract. |
| Ye and Steinhardt. "Norm enforcement for AI agents: robustly shaping behavior in multi-agent systems." ICML 2026 https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/76898 | 2026 | LLM agents exploit simple enforcement mechanisms. Reliability-tracking, escalating penalties resist this. | Partial. Same problem (norms plus misaligned agents). Capability levels not described in the page I read. |
| Reward hacking surveys and benchmarks: Springer survey https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44163-026-01980-z ; arXiv:2604.15149 (LLMs gaming verifiers) ; arXiv:2605.02964 (reward hacking benchmark) ; arXiv:2605.21384 (SpecBench) | 2026 | Report that more reasoning effort raised shortcut rates, and that larger models sometimes found more loopholes. | Partial. Capability and exploit finding rise together. None finds a threshold, and none compares against a model checker's prediction. |
| Skalse et al. (2022), "Defining and characterizing reward hacking" (recalled, NeurIPS 2022); Krakovna et al. (2020) specification gaming list (recalled). | 2020-22 | Theory: almost no proxy is unhackable. Catalog of gaming examples. | Background. Supports the premise, not the measure. |

Gap found: none of the retrieved work measures exploits found outside a formally verified model, nor pairs them with the model checker's own prediction, nor places models on a capability and cost ladder.

Narrowest surviving claim: "For a rule set proven safe in a stated formal model, we measure at what model tier and cost agents first reach a violation whose trace is not in the model's action space, and compare that with the checker's predicted exploit depth." Do not claim that capability thresholds for specification gaming are new. Scaling trends are already reported.

## 2. Empowerment to score norms or institutions; preserving others' empowerment

| Work | Year | What it does | Overlap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Du, Tiomkin, Kiciman, Polani, Abbeel, Dragan. "AvE: Assistance via empowerment." NeurIPS 2020 https://papers.nips.cc/paper/2020/hash/30de9ece7cf3790c8c39ccff1a044209-Abstract.html | 2020 | A helper agent maximizes the human's empowerment instead of inferring goals. Lunar lander user study. | Partial to high for "empowerment as an alignment objective". One human, one assistant. Not norms or institutions. |
| Ellis, Myers, Tuyls, Levine, Dragan, Eysenbach. "Training LLM agents to empower humans." arXiv:2510.13709 https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13709 | 2025 | Fine-tunes assistive LLMs to maximize the human's empowerment. Code assistance. 18-person study. | Partial to high for the same reason. Single principal. |
| Shah, Nemenman, Polani, Tiomkin. "Multi-agent empowerment and emergence of complex behavior in groups." arXiv:2604.21155 https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.21155 | 2026 | Extends empowerment to groups of agents, with other agents as interference. Physical systems (tendon pair, flock). | Partial. Multi-agent empowerment exists. Not used to score norms. No LLMs, no alignment. |
| Salge, Glackin, Polani (2014), empowerment overview, arXiv:1310.1863 ; Salge and Polani on empowerment and coupled agents (recalled, not checked). | 2013-14 | Intrinsic motivation and "stay empowered" ideas, some including other agents (recalled). | Possible partial overlap I did not verify. Check Salge's work on "empowerment as a measure for others" before claiming novelty. |
| Institutional-design papers for multi-agent AI safety, e.g. arXiv:2608.09828, arXiv:2602.13275, arXiv:2610.37342 | 2026 | Argue for institutions (separation of duties, audit) for reliable multi-agent behavior. Power-ability alignment. | Weak. Institutional framing, no empowerment score. Titles and summaries only. |

Gap found: I found no work that scores a norm set or institution by the empowerment each member keeps, or that treats "do not reduce others' empowerment" as a network-level metric alongside a cooperation score. This is the riskiest overlap overall, because AvE and Empower already use empowerment as an alignment signal for a helper and a human.

Narrowest surviving claim: "Empowerment, computed from the household transition system, as a per-member freedom score for a norm set, reported next to a cooperation score on a Pareto frontier." Credit AvE and Empower for empowerment as an assistance objective. Do not claim empowerment-for-alignment is new. Do not claim "collective empowerment" as a term. The UCSD framework (https://emerge.ucsd.edu/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/emerge-conceptual-framework-to-measure-empowerment.pdf) uses it in a social-science sense. Also check Turner et al. (power-seeking) for the "others' options" link. Their theory is single-agent.

Gap found: no hit for "actual quality via counterfactual re-runs of the same persona (alone, or with another partner), minus the agent's belief, as a misappraisal score, with switchable biases". Queries were few and the topic spans several fields, so this is weak evidence.

Narrowest surviving claim: "In a simulated household, misappraisal is the gap between an agent's belief and the quality of a counterfactual re-run from the same start, with biases as switches, used to compare norm sets." Fidelity to real people is a separate claim the project already declines to make.

Gap found: role-swap bisimulation is a known tool for anonymity and symmetry. I found no use of it to check that a household norm set treats roles equally. Equal constraints versus equal claims is our own distinction.

Narrowest surviving claim: "Role-swap bisimilarity as a check that a norm set imposes the same constraints on every role, including human and AI roles in peer mode." Present it as an application of a known technique. Do not present it as a new technique.
