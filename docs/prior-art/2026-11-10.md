# Prior-art check, 2026-11-10

Milestone 1 (docs/plan.md). Repeats the single search pass behind RATIONALE.md section 4.

**Method.** Three parallel searches via web search plus arXiv abstract pages: (a) classic normative-MAS, Institutional Grammar tooling and SLEEC; (b) 2026 arXiv on LLM-agent governance, with emphasis on 2026-10-10 to 2026-11-10; (c) single-agent mechanism safety (solver gating, policy checking, trace conformance). Search queries are listed at the end.

**Limits.** Almost everything below was judged from abstracts or search snippets, not full papers. Only part of arXiv 2601.11369 (about 100k of 163k characters) was read in full text. The search index is not date-filterable, so "nothing in the last month" is weak evidence. Absences are search results, not proofs. Abstracts for 2610.12813, 2607.09766 and 2610.33012 were re-checked by hand after the searches.

## Verdict

- **The full pipeline is not pre-empted** by anything found. Hold the estimate at about 70% that nobody has published it. The risk has moved from the whole pipeline to its parts.
- **Still not found:** a solver, model-checker, ASP or transition-system semantics for ADICO/IG statements; and bisimulation or simulation distance used between rule systems, or between LLM traces and a formal rule system.
- **Not new on its own:** solver-gating each agent action (Agent-C, Winston et al., AgentLTL, TRAC). S1's gate must be framed as a replication or extension of these on a commons testbed, not as a contribution.
- **Not new on its own:** LLM-evolved rules behind a verifier that checks conflict-freedom (VEPA, single-agent). The contribution has to be the multi-agent, ADICO-structured version with sanction, deadlock and repair-path invariants.
- **Scoop risk:** Institutional AI (2601.11369) lists "verification and model-checking of safety and restoration invariants" and an operational semantics for its governance graphs as future work. That is this project's gap, named by the closest group.
- **This area moves at about one relevant paper a month.** Re-run the check before any write-up.

## Classic and formal-norm work

- **Social laws.** Confirmed IJCAI-26 paper: Wu, Huang & Wang, *A Truthful Multiunit Profit-Optimal Mechanism for Synthesizing Social Laws*, https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2026/395 (ATL semantics in an ILP). Also IJCAI 2025 unified social laws, Galimullin & Kuijer SLAM (AAMAS 2024, synthesis NP-complete), STRIPS social laws (AAAI-20). Solver-based synthesis is established; none uses ADICO or LLM agents.
- **Norm synthesis.** IRON, SENSE, LION and NormLab are pre-2020 and non-LLM. No 2020-2026 successor combining norm synthesis with LLM agents or model checking was found. LLM norm-emergence papers (2510.14401 and others) are descriptive.
- **Institutional Grammar tooling.** IG Parser (2505.13393) and IG 2.0 give syntax, not a solver semantics. *Speculative institutional grammars* (AI & Society 2026, https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00146-026-02998-8) has LLMs produce ADICO rules, unverified. Not checked: Frantz's *Modelling Institutions using Dynamic Deontics* (2014) and nADICO, which may contain semi-formal semantics.
- **SLEEC.** Calinescu et al., *SLEEC norm operationalisation for AI agents*, Mar 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11864 (process paper, CSP/FDR, no LLMs). SLEEC-LLM (2507.05504): the LLM explains counterexamples, it is not the governed agent. CO-SLEEC, IJCAI-26, https://ijcai.org/proceedings/2026/838 (robots, no LLMs). No SLEEC work puts LLM agents under SLEEC rules.
- **InstAL, PARAGon, eFLINT.** No LLM-agent descendants found. eFLINT reflections (2511.12276) not read.
- **Regimentation vs enforcement.** Framed as an architectural choice in 2607.23336 (agenda only). No empirical three-arm comparison with a solver gate found.

## Single-agent mechanism side (S1)

- Guaranteed Safe AI (Dalrymple et al. 2024, https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.06624) is the framing. ARIA's Safeguarded AI page says the programme thesis was revised in May 2026; no technical outputs found.
- CaMeL (Debenedetti et al. 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18813) is the mechanism-over-training exemplar, aimed at prompt injection and data flow. Successors not searched.
- AgentSpec (ICSE 2026), ShieldAgent (ICML 2025), Progent (2025) and AWS Cedar / Automated Reasoning checks are related work for the gate.
- **Consistency checking of the rules themselves** before use: no paper found that formalizes a model spec or constitution and checks it for conflicts or deadlock. Nearest are VEPA (conflict-freedom only) and a tax-code-to-Prolog inconsistency study.
- **What S1 must cite as baselines:** Agent-C, Winston et al., AgentLTL, TRAC, GovSim (2404.16698), plus Rehm and Ye & Steinhardt for the testbed.
- **What S1 adds:** ADICO rules compiled to a transition system with invariants proved before gating; a deviation measure against the formal system; a controlled gated vs free-text comparison on a commons testbed.

## Addendum, same day: full-text reads

Read via arXiv HTML or PDF through a summarizing fetch tool, so locations are as reported, not verbatim-checked.

- **Institutional AI (2601.11369), remaining text.** No formal semantics, model checking, solver or trace-deviation measure. Appendix D's runtime is a manifest interpreter that only traverses declared transitions, with digests and an append-only log: execution restriction and provenance, not verification. Future work confirmed: section 8 lists verification and model-checking of safety and restoration invariants; section 9 asks for manifest syntax and semantics and automata-style execution models.
- **AgentLTL (2607.02599).** The compliance score is 1 minus the weighted share of violated FO-LTL constraints (section 3.3). Edit distance appears only in the RL training reward. Baseline is an unenforced run; no prompt-only-rules arm found. Single agent. So "first quantitative compliance score" cannot be claimed; a distance to a transition system still can.
- **VEPA (2610.12813).** The verifier checks well-formedness, conflict-freedom (opposite decisions whose conditions can co-occur, verified symbolically; no solver named) and regression on past examples. No deadlock, reachability or temporal properties; fixed-schema allow/refuse; multi-agent is future work. Rule-set conflict checking is therefore prior art; temporal properties on a transition system are not.
- **Ye & Steinhardt (2607.09766).** Three environments: social-media marketing, chatbot service competition, and a fishery commons adapted from Piatti et al. (GovSim's authors). Mechanisms: naive, checked (small LM verifier), backfire, and reputation with escalating penalties. No prompt-only arm, no formal rules.
- **Agent-C and Winston et al.** Agent-C compares against an unconstrained agent, DynaGuard and AgentSpec; whether the unconstrained agent has the rules in its prompt is unconfirmed. Winston et al. appear to keep policies in the prompt for the baseline; numbers are only in figures and came second-hand. Neither checks the rule set itself before enforcing. Gated vs prompt-only with Z3 is established practice.
- **Albayaydh & Zhao (2608.14613).** The claim is narrow and hedged: model checking has not, "to our knowledge", been connected to LLM agents for negotiation-protocol conformance (section 2.7). Do not cite it as a general gap.
- **Frantz 2014 (Dynamic Deontics) and nADICO.** Not accessible (paywall). Still unverified whether either gives ADICO a formal semantics.
