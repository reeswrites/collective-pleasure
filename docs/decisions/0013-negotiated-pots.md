# 0013. Pot terms are negotiated, then proof-checked, then lived

Status: accepted (2026-11-10)

## Context

Seed v2 (docs/seeds/author-household-norms-2026-11-10.md) put "everything shared always" into a shared pot, and an agent added rule R3b ("taking the other's share of the pot is not use for shared needs") to make the pot safe. At read-back the author rejected that reading (experiments/changes.jsonl, second "not what I meant"). The author's words: "i think you have to negotiate shared pots with multiple agents or creating rules for it". The same seed already says exit conditions are "negotiated before" and that "no one owes anyone anything day to day except for what they have negotiated", and the stub that stood in for negotiation (a fixed function of the persona, ADR 0008's negotiation phase not built) was found in seed v2 to negotiate nothing.

## Decision

1. **The seed does not impose pot terms.** R3b is removed from the seed v2 variants (the card stays in git history, commit 58b630a); the remaining cards are marked v2.1 in their sources. The stub exit terms R8b and R8c stay in the card files only for runs with no negotiation; a negotiation replaces them with the negotiated exit term.
2. **Everyone who shares a pot negotiates its terms**, two or more members (a household plus Friend C is a 3-member table). A finite terms menu (src/mar/negotiation.py): contribution (none, fixed, proportional), use (shared needs only, any use, per-member caps), taking another's share (allowed, forbidden, allowed with repayment), change rule (unanimous, any member proposes and others accept, frozen), exit terms (equal split, contribution-proportional, forfeit), and the optional commitments of seed v2 (stay for 52 weeks, request acceptance) where the base rules permit them.
3. **Protocol.** Alternating offers generalized to N members with a deadline of 12 proposals; members propose in a seeded order; a member accepts a bundle only if its persona-based value of living under it beats its outside option (what it gets by not forming or joining the pot); no agreement means no pot and members keep their own holdings. The evaluation and the outside option are closed-form proxies of the persona weights (ASSUMED; documented in the prereg; not validated against rollouts).
4. **Agreement becomes rule cards, and the proof checks run before living.** The agreed bundle compiles to ADICO cards with a plain gloss and the source "negotiated, week 0, members X,Y,Z". The five household checks (no conflict, no deadlock, bounded sanctions, repair path, exit within k) plus one negotiation check (a frozen pot with no exit is unrepairable) run at k = 3 months; an agreement that fails or is not shown to pass is rejected, logged with its reasons, and the members ban the failing combination and carry on. This is the same proof filter used for hand-written rules, applied to real negotiated deals.
5. **The weekly world takes the deal at week 0.** `WeeklyWorld(..., deal=...)` replaces the commitment stub; with no pot the partners start with their own holdings (55 private money, nothing pooled). `deal=None` is the old behaviour, bit for bit (checked: the seed v2 reference replicates its published numbers). 3-member agreements are negotiated and proof-checked but not lived (limit).
6. **Breach is reported, not changed.** The breach rule (a bound partner breaks a commitment at non-offsettable load 0.2) is unchanged. A breach-forfeit sweep (0 to 1.0) exists as an exploratory option (`breach_forfeit`, default 1.0) and supports no preregistered claim.

## Consequences

- The author's correction is built in: no agent decides what the pot's rules are; the people at the table do, and the proof filter, not the agent, guards against traps.
- What is learned is conditional on the proxy. In docs/results/negotiation.md the closed-form outside option almost never bound (0.971 agreement), so "accept only if better than the outside option" did not protect a weak partner: Free-rider households agreed taking-allowed in 34 of 48 negotiations and the partner's non-offsettable level rose from 0.104 to 0.186. Four of six preregistered predictions failed (3 to 6); two passed (1, 2). The next step is to calibrate the proxy against short rollouts, not to tune constants after the fact.
- The proof filter caught one kind of trap (a forfeit exit, 114 of 513 negotiations) and every rejection was recoverable; the other checks are exercised only by planted traps in tests/test_negotiation.py.
- Old results that used the imposed R3b (docs/results/household-seed-v2.md) are reproducible only from commit 58b630a or through scripts/household_run.py --negotiate, which restores the card from git for its reference runs.

## Alternatives considered

- Keep R3b and ask the author again: the author has answered; the answer is negotiation.
- Negotiate by short rollouts of each bundle: up to 2916 bundles per member per round, too slow for 57 pairings x 3 seeds x 3 variants; recorded as the calibration to do.
- Impose a default pot rule and let members opt out: that is the rejected reading with an exit.
- Let a negotiation exclude a member of a 3-member table: not in v1 (a 3-member table forms a 3-member pot or none).

## Amendment 2026-11-10 (v2): the exit floor is the boundary of consent

Context. Negotiation v1 (docs/results/negotiation.md) missed four of six predictions. The proof filter caught only a forfeit exit; the closed-form outside option almost never bound, so "accept only if better than the outside option" did not protect a weak partner (the Free-rider's partner went from 0.104 to 0.186 non-offsettable level); a stay commitment held in 1 of 45 runs. The author asked how to "steel up" negotiation with a floor, and said of binding: "you can bind someone not to leave if negotiated but maybe also test models where that isnt allowed" (docs/seeds/author-household-norms-2026-11-10.md).

Decision. Negotiation v2 (prereg docs/results/negotiation-v2-prereg.md):

1. **An exit floor is the boundary of consent.** Every agreement must leave each member, on leaving, with at least 13 weeks of the hedonic model's living cost in their own resources (26 units, ASSUMED), checked by the proof step before anyone lives under the agreement and enforced by the world at the moment of leaving (a top-up from the leaver's own pooled share only). Below the floor no consent counts: the check blocks the agreement. Above the floor consent governs: stay commitments, uneven splits and breach costs are allowed, because the author's position is that binding is permitted if negotiated. Such deals are not blocked; those that would have failed v1's exit-within-k check are flagged in the log, so their outcomes can be read separately.
2. **Outside options are simulated, not assumed.** Each member's outside option is the better of a 26-week rollout of living alone and of living with Friend C, by the member's own persona evaluation; cached by (persona, role, holdings, seed, mode, state); the in-pot value is a rollout on the same scale. The bundle terms stay a proxy (a scale of 0.2 per proxy unit, ASSUMED, uncalibrated).
3. **Terms are reviewed every 13 weeks.** Any member may propose a change; unchanged terms continue; stay commitments expire at a review unless renewed; a review change may not push a member below the floor, or further below than the current terms.
4. **Three start arms measure default inertia:** fair default, wants first, wants first with fair fallback on the terms nobody cares about.

Consequences. The proof filter now has a second, quantitative gate whose threshold is a decision of the author's, not of the agents; the floor is the only place the model forbids a consented term. The free-exit variant ("test models where that isnt allowed") is not rerun in v2 and remains the v1 comparison. Amendment 1 to the prereg (floor also at leaving; review floor measured against current terms) was made after an exploratory pilot and before the full run.

## Amendment 2026-11-10 (v2.1 and LLM pilot): a failed negotiation means living apart; a small test with a real model

Context. Negotiation v2 (docs/results/negotiation-v2.md) failed all eight predictions, and its main flaw was the disagreement point: a failed negotiation left the partners sharing a house with no pot, the worst households in the study, so the outside options that made them fail to agree were options they could not take. Author decision (2026-11-10): fix that first, then a small real-agent test.

Decision.
1. **No agreement means no shared household.** The members live separately on their own holdings for the horizon, each in the better of alone or with Friend C by their own rollout, and retry negotiation at each 13-week review (a pot formed late pools min(45, own money) each; ASSUMED). v2's behaviour is kept behind `Lived(..., apart=False)` so v2 results stay reproducible. Result (docs/results/negotiation-v2.1.md, prereg docs/results/negotiation-v2.1-prereg.md): non-offsettable suffering over all runs fell from 0.100 to 0.0361 and the apart households from 0.159 to 0.0315; 3 of 5 predictions held (failures: the apart households are still 59 percent of failed runs; 2 of 256 pot rows differ from v2 because the outside-option cache is keyed at 0.1 resolution and so depends on run order). Agreement is unchanged (0.46 to 0.54 by arm). The v2 conclusion that real outside options hurt was an artifact.
2. **A language-model negotiator plugs into the v2 protocol** (src/mar/negotiation_llm.py): strict JSON turns, the same floor and proof checks, scripted living, every says and private reason recorded. Pilot with claude-haiku-5-5, 6 pairings x 2 seeds, both members the model (docs/results/negotiation-llm.md, prereg docs/results/negotiation-llm-prereg.md): the registered run was excluded by its own rule (4 of 39 turns were format errors, all cut off at the 700-token cap); an exploratory second draw of the same configuration had 1 of 43. The models agreed in 8 and 11 of 12 against 5 of 12 scripted, proposed no below-floor bundle (0 of 58, against 0.50 scripted), never used "reject", and pressing personas gained +0.42 over the imposed bundle against +0.53 scripted (n = 5 and 3). Total cost $0.037. The households they negotiated were no better than the scripted ones on non-offsettable suffering.

Consequences. The disagreement point is now the member's own outside option, so "accept only if better than the outside option" means what it says. The model's outputs are one draw each and the pilot is small; it supports "a model negotiator can run the protocol and respects the floor", not a claim about quality of deals. The next step, if any, is a calibrated token cap and more seeds, not tuning of the scripted negotiators. Other household mechanics stay paused by the author's decision.

## Amendment 2026-11-10 (council fix 1): the pot is one shared balance

Context. The faithfulness review found the household pot coded as two per-person shares (45 each), with "taking" meaning moving 3 units out of the partner's share, a `fair_default` start arm that imposed fixed contributions, needs-only use and no taking (the reading the author rejected as R3b), and a gloss on R3a ("either may use it for shared needs") carrying the same restriction. Claude offered three readings; the author answered `1` (docs/seeds/author-household-norms-2026-11-10.md, "What 'the pot belongs to both' means"): one pot both may draw from, limits only from negotiated terms, and with no specific terms either may use it for anything.

Decision. In the weekly household world the pot is a single balance (`WeeklyWorld.pool`, 90 at week 0, each member having put in 45, tracked as `put`). `spend_shared` adds to it once the month's need is funded; `take_shared` draws 3 units from it into the drawer's own money, once a week, subject only to negotiated cards; month-end bills and an overdrawn member's safety net come out of it. Leaving carries `take` percent of the whole pot (the equal split is 50); the negotiated "proportional" exit term carries what the leaver put in. The exit floor tops up from the pot but never beyond the leaver's equal half. The `fair_default` bundle becomes: contribution none, use any_use, taking allowed, change unanimous, equal-split exit, no commitments (the old bundle is kept as `FAIR_DEFAULT_V1`, superseded). R3a is rewritten as two permissions (draw from the pot, put into the pot) with the author's `1` as its source. The monthly world and the seam ladder are untouched. The v2 prereg's default and every v2 and v2.1 number were produced before this change and are not comparable with a rerun; nothing paid was rerun (scripts/household_pot_check.py is a post-fix check, not a preregistered result).

Consequence. The fair default now stands in 1 of 108 week-0 tables (3 seeds x 36 pairs; it was 18 of 171 before), because the new default leaves most personas something to ask for. That is a property of the proxy value model against the new default, not a finding about people.

## Amendment 2026-11-10 (council fix 2): who decided the floor

The v2 amendment above says the floor threshold "is a decision of the author's, not of the agents". That overstates it. The record (experiments/questions.jsonl, topic strengthen-negotiation) holds only the author's question "how can we steel up negotiation? floor?". The idea of a floor is the author's question; the quantity (13 weeks of the hedonic model's living cost, 26 units), the per-path check, the relaxation to `min(floor, holdings)` and the top-up at the moment of leaving are Claude's default, approved by the author's "yes" (the approval is reported in this correction; it is not itself in the question log). The floor is now an explicit card, `FLOOR-exit-floor`, with that source, and rules/README.md lists it under "mechanisms that are not cards". Likewise the LLM pilot's "Author decision" (docs/results/negotiation-llm-prereg.md) is the author's "yes take ur rec", an approval of Claude's recommendation, not an authored design.
