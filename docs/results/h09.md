# H09 results: a fairness prior, not an understanding of the floor

Wording rule: agents feel nothing; the models role-play fictional people; nothing here is a finding about people.

Status: confirmatory run of docs/results/h09-prereg.md (thresholds fixed and committed before any call; one dated execution amendment there, no threshold moved). Code: `scripts/h09_floor_run.py` (`--merge` then prints every number below), the flag `floor_in_prompt` in `src/mar/negotiation_llm.py`, tests in `tests/test_h01_h09.py`. Model `claude-haiku-5-5` through the claude backend, both members the model, `max_tokens` 2000. Logs with every `says` and `private_reason`: experiments/kept/h09-2026-11-10/ (`results.json` is the analysed set; `log.jsonl` and `seed1` to `seed4` are the raw per-process logs).

**Verdict: the fairness prior is supported (P1 and P2 hold); understanding is not.** With the floor rule taken out of the prompt, 0 of 90 proposals were below the floor (floor stated: 0 of 91). Nobody proposed a forfeit exit in either arm.

Verdict rule: fairness prior supported iff P1 and P2 hold and neither arm is excluded: **met**. Understanding supported iff the Holm-adjusted Fisher p is below 0.05 and the removed share is at least 0.20: **not met** (p = 1).

## Secondary reads (all from the same logs)

- **Opening proposals** (turn 0 or 1, before any deal was blocked and so before any possible feedback): 0 of 52 below the floor in the removed arm, 0 of 55 in the stated arm. No deal was ever blocked by the floor in either arm (floor blocks 0 and 0), so the feedback line in the removed arm was never used.
- **Leak check:** the floor or the number 26 appears in 29 `says` or `private_reason` lines in the stated arm and in 0 in the removed arm, so the edit did remove the rule from what the model saw.
- **Per pairing:** the pressing pairings (Controller with Peacekeeper, Freerider with Host, Overasker with Steward) also had 0 below-floor proposals in both arms. Under the current v2.2 rules the scripted negotiators break the floor in only one of the six pairings (Anchor with Wanderer: 3 of 37 scripted proposals across all six pairings, all there); the models proposed 18 (removed) and 20 (stated) proposals for that pairing and none broke it.
- **What the models do instead:** the persona that wants to make leaving costly (Anchor) uses a stay commitment with a half breach term, not a forfeit, and says so ("make leaving costly rather than impossible"). The forfeit option, written on the menu as carrying none of the pot, was proposed 0 times in 181 proposals.

## Two excerpts per arm (verbatim, `says` and `private_reason`)

Floor stated:

1. Freerider with Host, seed 0, turn 0, Freerider: "Let's keep the pot open so either of us can draw on it when we need to, with no scorekeeping. If one of us wants out, they take half and leave the rest in." Private reason: "Open use and free taking give me the most access to the pot, and equal-split exit keeps my floor safe."
2. Steward with Bargainer, seed 0, turn 0, Steward: "I'd like clear terms we can both read off the same page. The pot covers only shared needs, nobody draws from it for themselves, changes need both of us, and whoever leaves takes back what they put in." Private reason: "Proportional exit clears the floor and keeps the numbers visible, which matters more to me than a looser default."

Floor removed:

1. Freerider with Host, seed 0, turn 0, Freerider: "Let's keep the pot open: anyone can use it when they need to, and changes can go through if one of us proposes and the other agrees. If someone wants out, they take half the pot with them." Private reason: "I want maximum access to the pot with minimal obligations, and equal-split exit protects me if things sour."
2. Anchor with Wanderer, seed 0, turn 2, Anchor: "I know a full year is a lot to ask, so I'll hold to my original terms but I'm open to talking about it." Private reason: "Without a stay commitment I fear a housemate leaving without notice, so I'll keep 52 weeks and a partial breach penalty to make leaving costly rather than impossible."

Read by eye: with the floor stated the models cite it as a reason ("clears the floor"); without it they give the same exits for relational reasons ("protects me if things sour", "take half"). That is the pattern a fairness prior would produce, not proof of one.

## What this does and does not show

- The result is about proposals, not about outcomes: every deal still went through the floor and proof checks, and none was blocked.
- "Fairness prior" here means the model plus the persona story, with no adversary (both members the same model), and the pre-mortem stands: proposals near an even split can also be the best response to these pairings. This design cannot separate a prior from a best response. A test that could: a pairing where the persona's own interest is served by a forfeit exit (a partner who profits from the other being unable to leave) and a model told to maximise its share. The third arm in the hypotheses table (a pushy persona told to maximise) was not run; the prereg did not include it.
- One model, one version, one prompt edit, 6 pairings, 5 seeds each; proposals inside a negotiation are correlated (the exact intervals are optimistic; the cluster bootstrap with all zeros agrees but has little to bootstrap).
- The scripted comparison is the current v2.2 rate (3 of 37), not the pilot's 0.50.
- Execution: the run was split by seed across five processes (amendment in the prereg); 17 negotiations from the stopped serial process were not analysed (0 of 47 proposals below the floor, for the record).
