# H32-v2 and H32b-v2 results: on the fixed model, free exit moves the egoist, if the partner judges each week

**Verdict: H32 is supported on the fixed model, in a band.** 6 of 6 verdict predictions held (3 descriptive items reported). The pattern (exit plus amplification converge with the pair kept; neither alone does) holds at all 3 amplification levels where the reachability check said it could (alpha 0.75, 1, 1.25), at threats 0.4, 0.6 and 0.8: 9 cells, exactly the predicted set. The check had already shown, at an out-of-grid threat, which alphas could reach it, so the cell set was predicted with that knowledge.

## What changed in the model

- **Threat split from payoff.** The partner leaves below `o_leave` (swept 0.1 to 0.8). Living apart pays it `o_B` = 0.25 (fixed). In H32 these were one number, so a stronger threat also made splitting better for the utilitarian.
- **Companionship 0.1** per member per week together (upkeep stays 0.05). A bond is worth +0.05 a week to the egoist even with amplification and giving off.
- **The partner judges each week on its own** (mean over a window of 1 week). One week below the threat ends the pair. H32's smoothed estimate (weight 0.5) let the egoist alternate a selfish week with a generous one.

## Does exit now move the egoist?

Yes. In every pattern cell the egoist's weeks shift by 0.48 (TV distance between its amp-only and exit+amp occupancy): it drops its own passion and does common ground A, as the utilitarian does, and the pair stays together for all 52 weeks (the partner would leave only after the end-game week). The same holds with giving in place of amplification: exit+give converges with the pair kept at threats 0.25 to 0.6 (any alpha).

It moves the egoist the other way too. At alpha 0.25 and 0.5 the pair is not worth enough to the egoist under a strong threat, so it lets the partner go: at alpha 0.5, threat 0.4, R rises from 0.165 (amp alone) to 0.365, and the partner's share falls from 0.56 to 0.26. A threat helps a partner only when the bond is worth keeping to the other side.

## What the estimator result means

The H32 conclusion "exit does not move the egoist" came from the partner, not from exit. With a partner that averages over 4 weeks, the fixed model gives 0 pattern cells. With the H32 smoothed estimate, 2. With a partner that judges each week, 9. An egoist facing a partner who can be satisfied on average keeps its selfish weeks and pays them back. This is the H32b renegotiation result in another form: a rule that can be met on average gets gamed.

The full ladder fails for the same reason. One bad week moves the partner one rung up and the next good week moves it back down, so the egoist alternates forever. Its single cell at w 0.5 and w 0 (alpha 1.25, threat 0.4 or 0.8) is not exit at work: a partner that cannot see its own pleasure sits permanently on the withdrawal rung, which halves amplification and changes the egoist's best weeks.

## Robustness, corrected

Noise no longer wipes out the result: with the corrected criterion (the utilitarian keeps the pair with at least half the probability noise alone allows), 5 percent exit noise leaves all 9 cliff cells and all 8 withdraw-only cells. Partner blindness does wipe it out: the count falls with sight, to 0 for a blind partner. That is expected, since the threat works only through the partner's own pleasure. It is also the rigging the H32 pre-mortem named: the convergence is built on a partner whose exit reads its own pleasure.

## Plain-English reading

For the essay: an egoist does attend to its partner's pleasure when three things are true. The partner can leave. The partner judges what happens week by week, not on average. And the egoist gets enough from the shared life that losing it hurts. Take any one away and the egoist goes back to its own weeks, or lets the partner go. Solitary life still wins when the shared life adds little (alpha 0.5 and below, here).

For mechanism-based alignment: exit can make a self-interested agent act for another's welfare, but only when the agent values the relationship and the other side cannot be paid off on average. A voice process that de-escalates on any good week is gamed. A strong exit threat against an agent that does not value the relationship makes things worse for the protected party. All of this is one menu, a rule-following partner, and assumed numbers.

## ASSUMED and limits

All of the prereg's. The weekly-judging partner is close to a hard demand, and the result depends on it (D1). Companionship 0.1 and apart payoff 0.25 are assumed and not swept. The reachability check looked at the exit arms at one out-of-grid threat before the predictions were written.
