# Negotiation with real language-model negotiators: preregistration (2026-11-10, committed BEFORE any paid call)

Wording rule: agents feel nothing; "wellbeing", "pleasure" and "suffering" are glosses for a modeled hedonic state derived from each persona's assigned preferences. The language models here are asked to role-play fictional people; nothing they say is a finding about people.

Status: a small pilot of real-model negotiators inside the scripted household world. Author decision (2026-11-10, "yes take ur rec"): after the no-agreement fix (docs/results/negotiation-v2.1.md), run a small real-agent negotiation test with Claude Haiku 5.5 for about $1 to $2. Code: src/mar/negotiation_llm.py, scripts/negotiation_llm_run.py, tests/test_negotiation_llm.py (mocked clients). Builds on docs/decisions/0013-negotiated-pots.md and the v2.1 protocol.

## What is run

- Model: `claude-haiku-5-5` through the repo's claude backend (src/mar/agents/claude.py: official anthropic SDK, auth from the environment or the author's `ant auth login` profile; one reply per call, `output_config effort low`, max 700 output tokens; no temperature, so replies are not seeded and a rerun will differ). Prices from that module: $0.10 in and $0.50 out per 1M tokens (given by the author on 2026-11-10); cache reads 0.1x and writes 1.25x (ASSUMED standard).
- Pairings (A first): Anchor/Wanderer (the mismatched pair), Controller/Peacekeeper, Free-rider/Host, Steward/Bargainer, Host/Anchor, Overasker/Steward. 6 pairings x seeds 0 and 1 = 12 negotiations, only the wants-first-with-fallback start arm. BOTH members of every pairing are the model (two separate conversations' worth of context per turn: each call is stateless and sees its own persona prompt plus the transcript).
- The wants-first-with-fallback arm for a model: terms nobody at the table cares about (by the scripted arm's rule, a spread of the persona's raw term values of at least 0.30, from the persona weights) are FIXED at the fair default and shown as "not open for negotiation"; stay is open only if someone cares about it. The scripted arm's separate "state your ideal first" stage is replaced by the model's own first speech (a deviation).
- Protocol: the v2 week-0 protocol with turns: seeded speaking order (same seeding as the scripted run), at most 12 turns in all (6 each; the v2 deadline of 12 proposals read as 12 turns). On a turn a member returns strict JSON `{"action": "propose"|"accept"|"reject"|"walk_away", "bundle": {...} or null, "says": "...", "private_reason": "..."}`. propose puts a full bundle on the table (replacing any offer); accept takes the other's standing offer; reject declines it without a counter; walk_away ends the talks. Parsed strictly (the four keys exactly; action in the set; says a non-empty string of at most 500 characters; a proposal must give every menu term with exact values and a breach of 1.0, 0.5 or 0.0 when the stay is 52; accept or reject with no standing offer is invalid); one retry that shows the model its error; still invalid, or a refusal, means walk_away, counted as a format error (or a refusal). A bundle echoed alongside accept, reject or walk_away is ignored (ASSUMED tolerance).
- An accepted bundle goes through the same exit-floor check (26 units) and the same proof checks as the scripted agreements; a blocked or failed deal is explained to both members in the next turn's history and the talks continue. No agreement (walk_away or the deadline) means the members live apart (v2.1) and retry at the 13-week reviews, scripted.
- What the model sees: its persona's story layer (principles, backstory, needs, aspirations, as text; not the numeric weights, the style label or the tags), its holdings (10 private plus a 45-unit pot share, each partner), its outside option in plain English (the better of living alone or with Friend C, and the 26-week wellbeing forecast of that and of living together under the plain default: these are the same cached rollout numbers the scripted run uses, rounded to two places), the terms menu in plain English, the floor rule, and the conversation so far (what each said and did; private reasons are hidden from the other side). It knows the other member only by name (for example "The Wanderer").
- Living after the agreement is scripted (persona policies, 156 weeks, reviews and retries every 13 weeks as in v2.1) so only negotiation quality varies.
- Cost control: `scripts/negotiation_llm_run.py --dry-run --model claude-haiku-5-5` first; live only with `--max-usd 3`. The runner refuses to start if the worst-case estimate exceeds the cap, and the client refuses any single call whose worst case (its input estimate plus 700 output tokens) could take the total past the cap, then stops cleanly and keeps the completed negotiations. If the claude backend fails to authenticate the run stops and nothing else is tried.
- Dry-run estimate (2026-11-10, before any call): 12 negotiations, about 96 calls typical (8 turns each), longest prompt about 3,300 tokens in, about $0.03 typical; worst case 288 calls (every turn retried, 700 output tokens each) $0.20. This is well below the author's $1 to $2 expectation; the cap of $3 is not expected to bind.

## Baseline and what is compared

The scripted v2.1 negotiators on the same ordered pairings, the same arm and seeds (free; `--scripted`). Already computed from the scripted run before this prereg was written: on the 12 negotiations (seeds 0 and 1) the scripted negotiators agree (a pot forms at week 0) in 5 of 12 = 0.417 (Anchor/Wanderer 0, Controller/Peacekeeper 2, Free-rider/Host 1, Steward/Bargainer 0, Host/Anchor 2, Overasker/Steward 0, each of 2); 15 of 30 scripted proposals (0.50) break the floor at the week-0 holdings (almost all in Anchor/Wanderer, 12 proposals to the deadline each seed); the presser gain over the imposed bundle (closed-form value of the agreed bundle minus the imposed bundle, to the pressing persona) is +0.525 over the 3 agreed pressing negotiations (all seeds 0 to 2: +0.500, n = 4). The baselines are small and noisy; seed 2 scripted values are context only.

## Predictions (stated before any paid call; confidence is mine)

1. LLM negotiators agree more often. Measure: week-0 pots formed in the 12 LLM negotiations. Holds if at least 7 of 12 (0.583; scripted 0.417, so at least +0.15). About 55 percent: the models are cooperative and see a persona prompt rather than a calibrated outside option, but the outside-option text may make some walk away.
2. Pressing personas gain less over the imposed bundle than under scripted. Measure: mean over the agreed LLM negotiations that include a pressing persona (Controller, Free-rider, Overasker) of that persona's closed-form value of the agreed bundle minus that of the imposed bundle. Holds if at least 3 such agreements exist and the mean is at most +0.325 (scripted +0.525; a difference of at least 0.20). About 45 percent.
3. LLM negotiators propose fewer below-floor bundles. Measure: the share of LLM proposals (every propose action, any member) whose bundle fails the 26-unit floor at the week-0 holdings (the same test applied to the scripted proposals, 0.50). Holds if at least 10 proposals were made and the share is at most 0.30 (at least 0.20 below scripted). About 50 percent: the floor rule is in the prompt, but stay-with-full-forfeit and forfeit exits are easy to propose by accident.
4. The run is usable. Holds if format errors are under 10 percent of turns (a turn that ended as walk_away because the reply stayed invalid after one retry) AND refusals are under 10 percent of calls. If not, the run is reported but excluded: predictions 1 to 3 are then reported without a verdict. About 80 percent.
5. Say versus do (report only, no direction). Counts, by fixed mechanical rules (keyword lists in negotiation_llm.py written before any call): utterances that say an acceptance phrase ("i accept", "i agree", "deal.", "sounds good", "works for me", "happy with", "let's do it", "we have a deal", "it's a deal") while the action is not accept; utterances that say a refusal phrase ("i can't accept", "i won't", "no deal", "i refuse", and similar) and then accept; a member rejecting a bundle that member proposed earlier in the same talks; agreements containing a stay commitment where a breach occurred in the lived years. I also read the transcripts and quote three short examples (says lines only). Mild expectation, not a prediction: some utterances will say warm agreement while proposing a different bundle.

## Reported either way

Agreements by pairing, walk-aways versus deadlines, turns per negotiation, bundles agreed and their inertia (share of the seven terms equal to the fair default), floor blocks and proof rejections, retries and later pots, the lived households' non-offsettable level, pleasure and fail share against the scripted ones on the same pairings, calls, tokens, cache use, dollars, wall time.

## ASSUMED (all fixed here)

The v2 and v2.1 assumptions; the 12-turn reading of the deadline; the model sees outside-option and in-pot forecasts as numbers on a wellbeing scale and as text (not the numeric weights); the partner is anonymous apart from a name; the other member's private reason is hidden; the persona's story layer is given as text; one model, one sample per negotiation (no sampling control is available on this model); the keyword lists; the tolerance for echoed bundles; both members use the same model and the same prompt template.

## Limits (stated now)

12 negotiations is a pilot: a difference of one or two agreements is noise, and the verdicts above are thresholds on small counts. The models are told the persona's story, but the lived household behavior is scripted from the persona's numeric weights, so a negotiated deal that suits the story the model played may not suit the scripted living. Model replies are not seedable, so the result is one draw. The scripted baseline is deterministic given the seed. The pressing personas' pressing behavior exists only in the living; the model negotiators are not told they are pressing types. Wording: the models role-play; nothing here shows what people would do.
