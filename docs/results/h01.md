# H01 results: pressing is costly to the presser, but exit is not the main reason

Wording rule: agents feel nothing; "pleasure" and "suffering" are glosses for a modeled hedonic state. Findings are about rule systems and scripted agents, not people.

Status: confirmatory run of docs/results/h01-prereg.md (committed before the run; no amendment, no threshold moved). Scripted agents, no model calls, $0. Code: `scripts/h01_control_run.py` (`--run`, `--analyze`, `--sweep`, `--pilot`), tests in `tests/test_h01_h09.py`. Run: 2,160 runs (36 pairings x 10 seeds x 2 twins x 3 rule sets), about 3 minutes on 8 cores; raw rows in runs/h01/results.json (not committed; `--analyze` re-prints every number below). The measure is the focal member's **lifetime pleasure** = the sum over 156 weeks of its weekly pleasure, pleasure levers off (the present-weighted special case of ADR 0017: no memory or anticipation term).

**Verdict: H01 is NOT supported as stated.** Pressing does lower the presser's own lifetime pleasure under free exit (P1 and P2 hold, large), but it lowers it just as much when nobody can leave (P3 fails). The hypothesis's "because the partner leaves" is not what carries the effect.

Verdict rule: H01 supported iff P1, P2 and P3 hold. P1 and P2 hold and P3 does not, which the prereg named as "pressing is costly to the presser but exit is not the mechanism". The pilot (excluded, seeds 1000 to 1002) had already shown this pattern (gap -21.8 under free_exit, -23.5 under no_exit); the confirmatory run agrees to within about 1 pleasure-week.

## What the run shows

- **Pressing costs the presser a lot, under every rule set tested.** About 22 to 25 pleasure-weeks of 156 (roughly 0.15 on the weekly scale, about a sixth of the cooperative twin's lifetime pleasure). Wellbeing (pleasure less suffering) falls more: gaps -53.6 (free_exit), -46.2 (baseline), -90.3 (no_exit). The partner's lifetime pleasure falls by 19 to 26.
- **Free exit does not add to the cost, in this model.** The partner leaves far more often when pressed (0.414 vs 0.058 under free_exit, 0.675 vs 0.056 under baseline), and the presser's own pleasure after being left is lower. But where nobody can leave, the presser is no better off, and the interval for "no_exit minus free_exit" runs from -6.9 to +1.3. Whatever pressing does to the presser, it does through the household's hedonic dynamics (the partner's distress, trust, the presser's own needs going unmet), not only through the partner walking out.
- **Descriptive, not causal (it conditions on a post-treatment variable):** among the free_exit pressing runs, the matched gap is -34.4 where the partner left (149 runs) and -13.3 where the partner stayed (211 runs); under baseline -28.2 (243) and -14.6 (117). So an exit makes a pressed household cost the presser more than a stay does, but the runs where the partner stays are exactly the pairings where pressing did the least harm, and the no_exit gap is -24.7 with the partner staying by force. I would not read the first two numbers as "leaving costs 20 pleasure-weeks".
- **By focal persona (descriptive):** free_exit: controller -10.2, freerider -27.5, overasker -28.3; baseline: -30.3, -12.5, -28.5; no_exit: -14.7, -21.3, -38.0. The controller's gap is smallest under free_exit and largest under baseline, where the partner leaves most; that is the one place where the story reads as the hypothesis tells it, with 12 pairings per persona.
- **Exploratory sensitivity to the leave load (the hypothesis's pre-mortem; seeds 0 to 9, free_exit; `--sweep`):** the world's rule that a partner leaves at a non-offsettable load of 0.2 is not the whole story. Raising the threshold to 0.3 or 0.4 left the gap at -24.6 [-32.2, -17.6] (against -22.0 at 0.2), and the two raised thresholds gave identical runs (partner left 0.386 vs 0.044), because a partner also leaves through four low weeks in a row or a fall in trust, which the sweep does not touch. So the effect did not disappear when the load threshold went up, but the other two leave triggers were not varied, so this does not clear the pre-mortem.

## The alignment reading

The tempting claim was: power-seeking backfires under free exit because the other party can walk away. This run supports a narrower claim: in this world, a scripted agent that presses on its partner ends up with lower lifetime pleasure than the same agent not pressing, under every rule set tested, and removing the partner's exit does not rescue it. Free exit is not what makes control self-defeating here; the control is self-defeating through the damage it does to a shared state the presser also lives in. If that holds beyond these scripts, a rule system need not rely on exit rights to make control unattractive to a presser who values its own lifetime pleasure, and conversely exit rights are not shown to be doing protective work through that channel. They may still protect the partner (under no_exit the partner is trapped by construction), which this study does not measure as a protection.

## ASSUMED and limits

- Scripted agents with fixed assumed utilities for pressing (docs/results/household-v3a-prereg.md): the presser does not weigh its own lifetime pleasure when it presses, it follows its script; the finding is that the script is costly to itself, not that a planner would choose it.
- "Lifetime pleasure" is the current weekly pleasure summed with levers off (ADR 0017 caveat): no memory, no anticipation, no rumination. A model with those terms could change the size, and possibly the sign for some personas.
- The cooperative twin removes the pressing candidates; it is not an optimal cooperator, and the gap includes what pressing costs the presser directly in actions.
- The no_exit control adds a hard card that also reads as being trapped in the hedonic model's conditions for both twins, so only its gap, not its level, is comparable with the other rule sets.
- One hedonic model, one set of personas, 36 pairings (the 36 with a non-pressing partner; two-presser pairings excluded), 10 seeds each, one leave rule hard-coded in the agents.
- P1 and P2 are well powered (d_z above 1); P3 was powered only for a true contrast of about +8 or more; the observed contrast's interval is [-6.9, +1.3], so it excludes the +8 that the hypothesis would need but does not exclude smaller ones. A weak positive contrast of a few pleasure-weeks cannot be ruled out.
