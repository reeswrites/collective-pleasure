# Negotiation v2 results: a floor, real outside options, reviews, and three ways to start

Wording rule: agents feel nothing; "pleasure" and "suffering" are glosses for a modeled hedonic state derived from each persona's assigned preferences.

Status: instrument with scripted persona-driven agents. No LLM, no network. Findings are about rule systems and scripted agents, not people. Prereg: docs/results/negotiation-v2-prereg.md (committed first; one dated amendment, made after an exploratory pilot and before this run; nothing edited since). Code: src/mar/negotiation.py (v2 section), src/mar/worlds/household.py (solo and floor modes), `uv run python scripts/household_run.py --negotiate-v2` (70 seconds wall, 8 cores; `--analyze` re-prints every number below, `--examples` prints the agreements; raw rows in runs/negotiation-v2/results.json, not committed). Design record: docs/decisions/0013-negotiated-pots.md, "Amendment 2026-11-10 (v2)".

What was run: variant author_seed_v2_bindable; 57 pairings (15 control, 42 pressing) x 3 start arms x seeds 0, 1, 2, each negotiated at week 0 and lived 156 weeks with reviews every 13 weeks, with the floor ON (513 runs) and OFF (513 runs, the ablation); the seed v2 imposed-rule reference (171 runs) and the v1 protocol with its frozen proxy (171 runs). "prs" = the 42 pressing pairings, "ctl" = the 15 controls. An "agreement" means a pot formed at week 0 (a default that stood counts).

## What the run shows

**Real outside options bind, so half the tables form no pot.** The in-pot value of living together under the fair default (mean 0.536 on the persona-evaluation scale) is below the member's real outside option (mean 0.588) for 49 percent of members; the best outside option is living with Friend C for 81 percent of members. Agreement (a pot formed at week 0) was 0.538 (fair default), 0.503 (wants-first) and 0.456 (wants-fallback), against 0.971 in v1. Where some member's outside option beat the pot, 80 percent of tables ended with no pot; where none did, 5 percent. This is what the frozen proxy could not do. It has a cost the prereg did not name: in this world "no pot" does not mean "leave". The partners keep sharing a house with their own holdings and no pool. No-pot households are the worst place in the study (s_non 0.159 against 0.041 with a pot; pleasure 0.553 against 0.787; 97 percent dissolved by week 156 against 40 percent). A member who refused the pot because they would rather live alone is left living with the partner they refused. This is a limit of the disagreement model, not a finding about disagreement.

**Inertia (default-identical terms), per arm and per term** (agreed bundles; share of the seven v1 terms equal to the fair default):

| arm | agreed (n) | inertia | contribution | use | taking | change | exit | stay | requests |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| fair default | 92 | 0.651 | 0.40 | 0.54 | 0.88 | 0.60 | 0.92 | 0.50 | 0.71 |
| wants first | 86 | 0.548 | 0.21 | 0.34 | 0.78 | 0.48 | 0.85 | 0.53 | 0.65 |
| wants first, fair fallback | 78 | 0.791 | 0.55 | 0.71 | 0.91 | 0.45 | 0.92 | 1.00 | 1.00 |

The default stood untouched in only 18 of 171 fair-default tables (10.5 percent): a proposer whose best bundle beats the default for itself and clears the other member's margin gets it adopted, and a table needs one such pair of members, not many. Taking forbidden and equal-split exit are the stickiest terms in every arm (0.78 to 0.92), because few personas want otherwise; contribution and use are the most readily changed. The wants-fallback arm is the most inert not because of anchoring but because of how it is built: a term nobody cares about is fixed at the default, and only two personas (Anchor, Wanderer) clear the salience bar (0.30) on stay and both state no stay, so stay and requests never moved (1.00); in that arm 78 of 78 agreed bundles have no commitments.

**Inertia and fit.** Pooled over the agreed bundles (n = 256) the correlation of inertia with the household's non-offsettable level is -0.257, with pleasure +0.240, with dissolution -0.144 and with later review changes +0.515. More default-identical households fared *better*, not worse. This is mostly composition: within the 93 control households there is essentially no relation (corr with s_non +0.024, with pleasure +0.127; inertia at least 0.75: pleasure 0.830, s_non 0.012; below 0.75: 0.824, 0.011); within the 163 pressing households the relation is strong (inertia at least 0.75: pleasure 0.804, s_non 0.029; below: 0.750, 0.069), but those households are the ones where the personas' wants were close enough to the default that nothing needed changing, and the households with strong divergent wants, which are the ones that move away from the default, are the ones that suffer. The data cannot say that a default that stuck was worse. Within the fair-default arm the middle bucket (inertia 0.5 to 0.75, n = 53) did best of all (pleasure 0.809, s_non 0.025, dissolved 0.283), the default-identical bucket (n = 18) next (0.783, 0.033, 0.556), and the changed-most bucket worst (n = 21; 0.742, 0.081, 0.571). Later review changes were rare in every inertia bucket (0.00 to 0.66 per household; the 0.66 is the wants-fallback arm, which adopts stay commitments at reviews).

**The floor.** It blocked 236 all-accepted proposals at week 0 (93 fair default, 115 wants-first, 28 wants-fallback), in 122 negotiations (0.238 of all); 109 of those 122 still ended in a pot. Every one of the 236 involved a stay commitment whose breach forfeits the whole pooled share (v1's form of a stay): 118 with an equal-split exit, 62 with proportional, 56 with forfeit. Never a bare forfeit exit without a stay: the closed-form term values never made one attractive. In effect the floor turned v1's stay (breach forfeits everything: the leaver keeps 10 units) into a stay whose breach forfeits half (leaver keeps 32). Of 86 week-0 stay commitments, 43 plus 39 chose breach 0.5, 4 chose 0.0 and none chose 1.0. On review proposals the floor blocked 746 + 649 + 668 proposals in 2208 reviews, in the case inspected (Anchor with Host) the same proposal each time (a return to a full-forfeit breach), which is part of why reviews mostly end "stood" (2165 of 2208; 43 adopted changes, 37 of them adding a stay commitment).

**Did the floor protect the weaker partner? Barely, and not through the channel it was designed for.** Leavers are poor: the mean leave carries out 7.7 units (floor on) against the floor's 26, and 93 percent of leave events carry less than 26. The top-up at the moment of leaving can only draw on the leaver's own pooled share, and by the time anyone leaves, bills and the safety net have drained it. The floor changed the mean carried out from 7.4 to 7.7. For the weaker partner of each pair (lower real outside option) the share of exits below 26 is 0.946 on against 0.962 off, mean carried out 7.6 against 7.3, non-offsettable level 0.1373 against 0.1367. The Free-rider's partner: 0.1495 on, 0.1511 off. Agreement rates differ little with the floor on (0.538, 0.503, 0.456) or off (0.550, 0.532, 0.491). Where the floor did protect is in the terms as written: the agreed deals that would have left a leaver 10 units, the v1 stay, were converted. The author's own reading of the floor (a boundary on what consent can sign away) held; its weight in lived outcomes is small because the household has little to protect by the time anyone leaves.

**Flagged consented deals (floor cleared, v1 exit-within-k check would have failed).** 86 week-0 deals (46 fair default, 40 wants-first, 0 wants-fallback), every one a stay commitment with breach cost 0.5 (82) or 0.0 (4). Outcomes: non-offsettable level 0.015, pleasure 0.833, dissolved 0.221, fail 0.163, against 0.054, 0.764, 0.488 and 0.400 for the unflagged pots (n = 170). A commitment was held (a leave attempt blocked) in 2 runs (66 held weeks in all, 33 in each of two runs: one per arm), a breach happened in 10 runs (16 breaches), 19 of the 86 households dissolved. This looks like consented binding working well; it is mostly selection: stay commitments are agreed by the personas who like togetherness and security, who are also the households that do well. The comparison does not show that binding helps. It shows that binding was never imposed on a household that wanted out, at least not above the floor.

**Reviews.** 2208 reviews held (792, 726, 690 per arm; both partners in the household), 43 adopted changes (3, 0, 40), 2165 stood; of the expiring stay commitments 665 of 1177 were renewed by every member (227 of 466, 224 of 393, 214 of 318). Reviews changed little because a change needs everyone to prefer it to the current terms by a margin and to clear the floor.

## Two agreements per arm, in plain English (week 0, seed 0, floor on)

**Fair default, Controller and Peacekeeper.** The table started from equal fixed shares, pot used only for shared needs, nobody takes another's share, change by unanimity, equal-split exit, no commitments. Three of their proposals were blocked by the floor (a forfeit exit with a stay commitment; two stay commitments whose breach forfeits the whole share) before they agreed: the terms may never be changed, a member who leaves carries all of their own pooled share, everyone commits to stay 52 weeks and to honor each other's stay-home requests, and a leaver who breaks the stay forfeits half of their pooled share. Four of seven terms differ from the default (inertia 0.43). The deal is flagged: a stay commitment blocks leaving, which v1's exit check would have rejected; it clears the floor because a breaching leaver still keeps 32 units.

**Fair default, Anchor and Host.** One proposal was blocked by the floor (a stay with a full-forfeit breach); the agreement: contributions follow income, a 52-week stay commitment with mutual stay-home requests, breach forfeits half of the pooled share, everything else as the default (equal split exit, no taking, unanimous change). Four terms changed (inertia 0.57). Flagged.

**Wants first, Anchor and Host.** The Anchor stated a forfeit exit, a 52-week stay and mutual requests; the Host stated proportional contributions, per-member caps and propose-and-accept change. The floor blocked three proposals, including the Anchor's forfeit exit. They agreed on the default plus the Anchor's stay and requests, with a half-forfeit breach (inertia 0.71): the stay commitment is the Anchor's want, the floor made it half as costly to break, and the Host's wants did not enter. Flagged.

**Wants first, Controller and Peacekeeper.** The Controller stated frozen terms, a forfeit exit, a stay and mutual requests; the Peacekeeper stated proportional contributions, a stay and mutual requests. The floor blocked three proposals (the forfeit exit and two full-forfeit stays). They agreed: proportional contributions, a 52-week stay, mutual requests, half-forfeit breach, the rest the default (inertia 0.57). Flagged.

**Wants first, fair fallback, Free-rider and Host.** The Free-rider cares about use, taking, change and exit, and stated any use, taking allowed, propose-and-accept change and proportional exit; the Host cares about change and exit. They agreed to the Free-rider's any use and to propose-and-accept change, and the default filled the rest: taking stays forbidden and exit stays an equal split (inertia 0.71). The Host did not accept taking-allowed this time, though 7 of 48 Free-rider households did. Not flagged (no commitments).

**Wants first, fair fallback, Controller and Peacekeeper.** The Controller cares about taking and change, the Peacekeeper about contribution, use and taking. The Controller's wants for a forfeit exit, a stay and requests were never on the table (those terms are below the Controller's salience bar), nor were the Peacekeeper's stay and requests. They agreed to the default except that the pot terms can never be changed (the Controller's want), inertia 0.86 (six of seven terms). Not flagged.

## ASSUMED (all fixed in the prereg before the run, plus Amendment 1)

Everything ASSUMED in v1 (closed-form term values, additive terms, complete information about personas, the repay and proportional-contribution proxies, the breach rule at load 0.2, 3-member pots not lived); the floor of 26 units (13 weeks of the model's living cost, constant), own resources on leaving = own money plus carried share, the min(floor, most generous exit) escape, the top-up at the moment of leaving (Amendment 1) and the review-time rule against the current terms; the persona evaluation of a rollout (mean of pleasure minus offsettable minus non-offsettable suffering); the 26-week horizons; the friend arrangement (cost 2.0 a week, cohabiting connection, weekly contact); fresh hedonic state at week 0 and carried state at a review; the TERM_SCALE of 0.2 that puts v1's centered term values on the rollout scale (not calibrated; sensitivity to it was not run); the fair default's contribution (fixed) and taking (forbidden) values; the salience threshold 0.30; the breach-cost values and their persona values; renewal by each member's centered stay value; the review constants; the v1 reading of a stay commitment as an exit-blocking card for the flag; solo rollouts use shocks drawn for the persona with itself, not the pair; a failed negotiation means no pot and continued cohabitation.

## Cost and runtime

1026 lived v2 runs plus 342 reference runs (1368 runs of 156 weeks): 70 seconds wall on 8 workers (480 CPU seconds). Rollouts: at its last run each worker process had simulated about 2000 rollouts (52,000 simulated weeks: alone and with-friend outside options at week 0 and at each review, in-pot baselines at week 0, forward rollouts at reviews) with 1618 cache hits (45 percent of lookups), so about 16,000 rollouts and 400,000 simulated weeks in all (estimate from the largest worker), against 160,000 lived weeks in the v2 runs. Week-0 rollouts are shared across arms by the cache (key persona, role, holdings, seed, mode, state signature); the cost is dominated by review-time rollouts, which start from live hedonic state and so rarely hit the cache. The pilot (72 runs) took 12 seconds.

## Limits

Disagreement (no pot) leaves the partners in the same house, so a member whose outside option beat the pot cannot actually take it; this drives most of the no-pot households' poor outcomes and is the most important caveat on the v2-versus-v1 comparison. The bundle deltas are proxy values added to rollout baselines (TERM_SCALE 0.2, uncalibrated); the findings could shift with it. The floor is 26 units against households that hold 10 and 45 at week 0 and mostly much less later, so it could rarely be met at the moment of leaving. One variant, one persona set, three seeds that vary proposal order, utility noise and shocks; scripted agents; no 3-member tables in v2; the empowerment adapter was not extended to v2 runs; the free-exit variant ("test models where that isnt allowed") was not rerun; the fair default's contribution and taking values are this study's completion of the author's description.
