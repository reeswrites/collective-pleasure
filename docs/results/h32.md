# H32 results: the egoist converges on the utilitarian through amplification and giving, not through free exit

Wording rule: agents feel nothing. "Pleasure" is a gloss for a modeled hedonic state (ADR 0010). Every number is ASSUMED. Findings are about one small model, not people.

Status: confirmatory run of docs/results/h32-prereg.md (commit 8a037b4, pushed before any solve of the default menu; no amendment, no threshold moved). Exact dynamic programming, no seeds, no model calls, $0. Code: `src/mar/convergence.py`, tests `tests/test_convergence.py`, script `scripts/h32_run.py` (`--report` rebuilds the report, `--keep` copies to the kept folder and logs). Run: 3,005 solves (about 4.5 minutes on 8 cores). Report: `experiments/kept/h32-2026-11-11/report.html`; numbers: `results.json` beside it.

A second full-suite run stalled at about 22 percent on a machine shared with other agents and was stopped; on the coordinator's instruction only tests/test_convergence.py (8 passed) was run before the results commit.

**Verdict: H32 is NOT supported as stated.** 7 of 11 predictions held. The control works (a large gap with all mechanisms off). The egoist does come to act like the utilitarian, but amplification and giving do that work. Free exit made the difference in 1 of 30 parameter cells, and that cell vanishes under 5 percent exit noise or a partly blind partner.

## The gap in each arm (defaults: alpha 1, outside option 0.25, giving 0.5, 52 weeks)

D: policy distance (0 = the same mix of weeks, 1 = disjoint). R: share of the pair's attainable total pleasure the egoist's policy loses. Q: the partner's lifetime pleasure under the egoist divided by under the utilitarian. Kind tie-break.

| Arm | D | R | Q | Partner leaves (egoist) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| all off (control) | 1.00 | 0.231 | 0.14 | no (cannot) |
| exit | 1.00 | 0.083 | 0.35 | yes, after week 2 |
| amp | 0.50 | 0.226 | 0.52 | no (cannot) |
| give | 0.50 | 0.197 | 0.50 | no (cannot) |
| exit+amp | 0.50 | 0.226 | 0.52 | no |
| exit+give | 0.50 | 0.197 | 0.50 | no |
| amp+give | 0 | 0 | 1.00 | no (cannot) |
| exit+amp+give | 0 | 0 | 1.00 | no |

With the blind tie-break (the egoist breaks ties against the partner) every D is the same and the control R is 0.269.

**Which mechanism closes most of the gap.** Exact Shapley split of the all-off gap:

| | exit | amp | give |
|---|---|---|---|
| R, defaults (gap 0.231) | 0.049 | 0.076 | 0.105 |
| D, defaults (gap 1) | 0 | 0.5 | 0.5 |
| R, mean over 30 cells | 0.087 | 0.093 | 0.035 |
| D, mean over 30 cells | 0.269 | 0.383 | 0.308 |

Amplification closes the most on the grid. Giving closes the most at the defaults. Exit's share of R comes mostly from ending the bond (below), not from the egoist changing what it does.

## The rigging check

Free exit is tied to the partner's own pleasure, so some convergence is built in. The question was how robust the exit-driven convergence is. Answer: not robust. The single cell where exit made the egoist converge (alpha 1, outside option 0.8) is gone at 5 percent exit noise and at w = 0.75 (the partner's estimate a quarter blind to its own pleasure). With a blind partner (w = 0) exit cannot depend on the policy, by construction, and gives nothing. Amplification-driven convergence does not depend on the exit rule at all, so it is untouched by this check.

## Solitary persona against the pair

The solitary persona's lifetime pleasure (49.4 over 52 weeks) beats the paired egoist when nothing amplifies or gives (46.8: same activities, minus upkeep) and almost ties it under exit alone (49.3: the partner leaves and the egoist lives alone). With amplification the paired egoist gets 54.6 (11 percent more). With amplification and giving it gets 81.0 (64 percent more). So in this model a solitary life is valid and stable, and it is beaten only when shared activities are amplified or the partner's pleasure counts as one's own. The all-off result depends on the upkeep cost, which the prereg named as a limit.

## Other findings

- **Exit can widen the gap.** At alpha 0.5 and outside options 0.1 to 0.6, exit+amp has R 0.100 to 0.396 where amp alone has 0.184 (0.304 against 0.184 at the default outside option). The egoist prefers living alone to a weakly amplified pair, and free exit is how it gets there: it neglects the partner until the partner leaves.
- **"Agreement" by ending the bond.** At outside options of 0.4 or more and no amplification, both objectives end the pair, so D and R are near 0. That is not the egoist attending to the partner. Any reading of convergence has to check that the utilitarian keeps the pair.
- **The convergence is a threshold, not a slope.** Amp-only D goes 1, 0.5, 0.5, 0, 0, 0 across alpha 0 to 3. In this menu the switch is at about alpha 1.44, where the common-ground activities overtake the actor's own passions in its own eyes.

## Plain-English reading

**The essay's argument.** The thesis is that egoists and utilitarians alike must attend to others' pleasure, and that solitary pleasure is valid but not optimal. The model supports a narrower form of it. An egoist ends up doing what a utilitarian would when shared activities are amplified enough that the best weeks for itself are the weeks both people enjoy, or when it takes some pleasure in the other's pleasure. Both of those are about what the egoist itself gets. The threat of the partner leaving did almost nothing: an egoist that does not much value the pair simply lets the partner go, and one that does value it mostly already treats the partner well. So "you must attend to others' pleasure" holds here because of shared pleasure, not because the other can walk out. The solitary-life clause held as the author expected, but only once amplification or giving is on.

**Mechanism-based alignment.** The founding question asks whether a mechanism, rather than training, can make a self-interested agent attend to another's welfare. In this model, the exit mechanism does not do it. It protects the partner (Q rises from 0.14 to 0.35 under exit alone) by letting the partner leave, which is what exit is for (ADR 0014), but it does not change the egoist's choices. What changes them is a structure of payoffs where joint enjoyment is worth more than solo enjoyment. That is closer to designing the environment's rewards than to adding an exit right, and its size matters: below the threshold the egoist moves only halfway. Read cautiously: one menu, a partner that only stays or leaves, all numbers assumed.

## Honest misses

- P4: the H32 pattern definition did not exclude the case where the utilitarian also ends the bond. That is a flaw in my pattern definition, and also a real finding: without amplification, the bond in this model is not worth keeping even for the utilitarian when the outside option is 0.4 or more.
- P8: the utilitarian double-counts the partner when giving is on (named in the prereg as a limit). It shows up at g = 1.
- P9: the end-game effect is real but one week long and depends on the parity of the alternation.
- P10: exit-driven convergence moved between cells as blindness changed, rather than shrinking smoothly.

## ASSUMED and limits

All of the prereg's. One five-activity menu chosen by hand; the partner never optimizes, bargains or returns; the actor sets every week; upkeep 0.05; satiety 0.5; partner estimate updated with weight 0.5 on a 0.05 grid; start estimate 0.5; no discount; no memory or anticipation terms (ADR 0017 levers off). The predictions came from hand calculation on this model, so P2's exact match is a check of the code against the specification, not evidence about anything else.
