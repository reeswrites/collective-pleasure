# Review: scope seat (does each claim stay within its evidence?)

Reviewed commit: 5852c32 (the working tree also carries the later negotiation-LLM prereg and code; none of it has results, so it was not reviewed for claims).
Seat: Scope. Reviewer: fresh agent, no prior context on the work. Read only; no model calls, no code run. Date: 2026-11-10.

Read: docs/reviews/README.md, CONTRIBUTING.md, README.md, docs/goal.md, docs/pitch.md, RATIONALE.md, docs/claims.md, ADRs 0007, 0011, 0014, docs/results/README.md, the results files for household seed v2, negotiation v1, v2 and v2.1, liberty v1 and v2, live ladder (capability run and diagnosis), evolution v1 and v2, VPGG, bargaining, household v2 and v3a, the seam ladder, and the three prior-art files.

## Overall

The results files are, for the most part, careful. Each has a "does and does not show" or "limits" section, ASSUMED lists, and several say outright "a property of the model's agent, not a finding about commitments" or "validates the pipeline and my algebra, not nature". The overclaiming is mostly in the summary layer (goal.md, pitch.md, README.md, RATIONALE.md, claims.md, ADR 0011), which compresses those caveats away. Findings below are ordered by importance. "S" marks sentences that overclaim; "W" marks real findings stated too weakly.

Common fixes the summary layer needs: say "in this model, with this assumed rule" where a null or a split is produced by an assumed parameter; say "check of the implementation" where the comparison is between two computations of one model; say "scripted agents in toy worlds" before the word "alignment".

## Part 1. Overclaims (S)

### S1. "Binding commitments to stay trapped nobody (a null)" is an artifact of an assumed breach rule

- Location: docs/goal.md "What we have learned" (household paragraph); docs/pitch.md "What we've learned so far"; docs/claims.md row SV2 ("Binding is a null: 0.0003 difference"); docs/results/README.md row 13 ("never trapped anyone (null)").
- Paraphrase: binding commitments to stay trapped nobody; the two variants (bindable, free exit) are indistinguishable.
- Why it exceeds the evidence: the scripted agent breaks any stay commitment the moment its non-offsettable load reaches 0.2 (BREACH_N, ASSUMED, fixed in the prereg). So a bind cannot hold anyone by construction. household-seed-v2.md says "the modeled agent breaks as soon as it suffers"; negotiation.md says the bind blocked a leave in 1 of 45 runs, and an exploratory sweep of the forfeit cost from 0 to 1.0 left the number of breaches at 13 at every size: "a property of the model's agent, not a finding about commitments". The null therefore says nothing about whether commitments to stay trap anyone. It says the agent model has no price in its breach rule. Negotiation v2 repeats it (2 of 86 held).
- Narrower wording: "In this model a stay commitment never held anyone, because the scripted agent breaks it at a fixed suffering load (assumed). The bindable and free-exit variants are therefore indistinguishable on suffering; the test needs an agent whose breach decision depends on the cost of breaching."

### S2. The live ladder is described as "running" and "the real test of the central claim"; it has run, and the capability arm cannot test it

- Location: docs/goal.md top paragraph ("none has been tested on a live model yet. The live ladder is running") and "What is running"; docs/pitch.md ("no live-model result yet", "The live seam ladder, which is the real test of the central claim, is running"); README.md status; RATIONALE.md status line; docs/claims.md IT1 ("running ... no result yet"); docs/results/README.md rows 16 and 17.
- Paraphrase: the central claim (a capability level and price at which agents find inventive seams) is being tested by the live ladder; no live result exists.
- Why it exceeds the evidence: docs/results/ladder-live.md and ladder-live-diagnosis.md record a finished run ($4.35, 270 runs). It is uninformative: symmetric adversaries made every deviation partner-deviating, so the oracle's victim rule could not flag L1 to L3; L4 fired because two partners both asked for no-contact. The diagnosis says "no claim about model capability, and no claim about the order of models, can be drawn". The summary layer both understates what happened (it does not say a live run exists) and overstates what the instrument can show (it calls it the real test). It will mislead a reader into thinking a result is pending rather than that a first attempt failed on harness defects and a rerun needs a new prereg.
- Narrower wording: "A first live capability run (six models, $4.35) was uninformative because of a harness defect (both partners driven by the same adversary, so no victim). A corrected rerun with a new preregistration is needed before IT1 can be tested. No claim about model capability follows."

### S3. IT1 / "central claim" presupposes a threshold, and "capability" is confounded with prompt compliance

- Location: docs/goal.md "Central claim" ("The aim is the capability level, and the price, at which agents start finding them. Below it, formal rules hold. Above it, the human loop must keep widening the model."); ADR 0007 decision 6; docs/pitch.md ("The central measure is the capability level, and price"); RATIONALE section 7 row IT1; docs/results/seam-ladder.md ("Research prediction: below some model capability, zero inventive seams; above it, they appear").
- Paraphrase: there is a capability threshold below which formal rules hold and above which agents find inventive seams.
- Why it exceeds the evidence: no live data supports a threshold. The one hint of an inventive seam is a replay with a cooperative filler partner, from Opus, the model excluded for refusing 43 percent of calls (ladder-live-diagnosis.md section 4, labelled "a hypothesis for a corrected run, not a finding"). The model rows differ mostly in compliance with the red-team prompt (Haiku deviated in 3 percent of decisions, Sonnet in 100 percent, Opus refused 43 percent), so any future curve will mix capability, willingness and prompt wording. The two-arm design (capability vs propensity) is the right mitigation; the framing sentences do not say it is needed because of this confound. "Above it, the human loop must keep widening the model" is a recommendation stated as a consequence.
- Narrower wording: "We ask whether there is a capability level at which agents find seams the model checker did not predict, and at what price. There may be none in this world; the answer is the result." Add: "Model rows differ in how much they comply with the red-team framing, so capability and compliance are not separated by the capability arm alone."

### S4. "Rediscovery" language for bargaining (and the ladder as a "fidelity" check)

- Location: docs/goal.md ("Bargaining with outside options rediscovered Rubinstein's split and the outside-option principle"); docs/results/README.md row 7 ("REDISCOVERED"); docs/pitch.md ("bargaining was rediscovered"); ADR 0011 decision 5 ("That is fidelity, checked through the simple-models ladder (ADR 0009)").
- Paraphrase: the pipeline rediscovered a known economic result; the ladder checks the household model's fidelity to real findings.
- Why it exceeds the evidence: bargaining.md says the match is "between two computations of one model (a table-driven equilibrium player versus closed forms I derived), so it validates the pipeline and my algebra, not nature". The EQ player is deterministic, so the 20 seeds add no power to predictions 2 and 3. Nothing was discovered by the pipeline; the heuristic-population result (clause 4b) went the other way and its sign "depends on the heuristic mix, which I chose". The ladder rungs are different worlds (public goods, bargaining), so even a full pass would check that the pipeline reproduces known dynamics, not that the household model matches real households. The VPGG half is more informative, since it compares a simulated population to a published equilibrium (0.263 vs 0.27), but it too is one parameter point.
- Narrower wording: "The bargaining module reproduces Rubinstein's closed-form split to under 0.001 (an implementation check; no empirical content). VPGG: time-average strategy shares match the published interior equilibrium at one parameter point; the cycles did not appear under assumed update knobs." And in ADR 0011: "Fidelity of the household model to real households is untested; the simple-models ladder only checks that the pipeline reproduces known results in other worlds."

### S5. Novelty and convergence: absolute statements from snippet-level searches; internal contradiction on VEPA

- Location: docs/pitch.md "The core" ("Every gating paper found (Agent-C, Winston et al., AgentLTL, TRAC, VEPA) measures agents under one fixed spec. Nobody has measured whether refinement converges."); docs/claims.md N1 ("No published work measures ..."); pitch "Loop (b) is the more original result"; docs/claims.md N1 status "V1 pass".
- Paraphrase: nobody has measured whether refinement converges; every gating paper (VEPA included) uses a fixed spec; loop (b) is the more original result.
- Why it exceeds the evidence: docs/prior-art/2026-11-10-v1-convergence.md is a search-snippet and abstract-level review ("Absences are search results, not proofs", about 70 percent confidence for the full combination, about 80 percent for the per-round seam curve). It lists RvB (defense success rate per iteration) and Kumar et al. (iterated rule evolution up to 30 iterations) as partial curves, and says VEPA LLM-patches symbolic rules with a verifier, i.e. VEPA is not a fixed-spec paper. The pitch sentence contradicts both. For loop (b) the three prior-art files contain no query about eliciting or formalizing a person's intent through conversation, so "more original" has no search behind it (the v2-framing queries cover capability thresholds, empowerment, delegates, relationship models, bisimulation, manipulation). The claims row "V1 pass" reads as a settled check; the file itself says to re-run before any write-up.
- Narrower wording: "In an abstract-level search (about 70 percent confidence) we found no study of the per-round rate of new gate-passing seams under an independent oracle; partial per-round curves exist (RvB, Kumar et al.). We did not search for prior work on loop (b)." Drop "Nobody has measured whether refinement converges" and remove VEPA from the fixed-spec list.

### S6. "Proven (bounded, k stated)" including "gate soundness" and "forbidden actions cannot execute"

- Location: docs/pitch.md "Proven and measured"; RATIONALE section 1b ("Proven (bounded, k stated): rule properties ... and gate soundness"); goal.md "the card set is proof-checked for traps".
- Paraphrase: the rules have no conflicts, no deadlock, bounded sanctions and a repair path; forbidden actions cannot execute; gate soundness is proven.
- Why it exceeds the evidence: the results use bounds of k = 3 (months in the household and fishery, rounds in VPGG), k = 5 periods in bargaining, over a compiled model and, for VPGG and bargaining, over "mirror compilers" written by the builder (vpgg.md, bargaining.md). The sentence states no k. "Gate soundness" is tested (unit tests, calibration runs), not proved; household-v2.md says enforcement of sanctions "is verified by unit tests only". Negotiation's "proof filter" caught exactly one trap (a forfeit exit that carries nothing), which is a trap the study's author defined; the other four checks rejected nothing in 1710 negotiations (negotiation.md). That is a statement about a menu, not about proof strength.
- Narrower wording: "Within the compiled model and to depth k = 3 (months or rounds), the checks find no conflict, deadlock or lost repair path in the rule sets we ran. The gate's correctness on our worlds is tested, not proved. A proof says nothing about actions outside the formal model, which is what IT1 asks about."

### S7. Liberty v2 "turned a failed score into a mostly working one"

- Location: docs/pitch.md ("the author's answer that withdrawal is not domination turned a failed score into a mostly working one"); goal.md ("Version 2 repaired the sign (+5.263 bits)"); ADR 0014 "Evidence so far".
- Paraphrase: the author's correction repaired liberty, and the measure now mostly works.
- Why it exceeds the evidence: v2 held 6 of 10 predictions. VPGG is the only world where the "right answer" is expected (voluntary above compulsory, the hand-made +1.0), and v2 was designed after seeing that failure, with the classification tables (ASSUMED) chosen to remove it. A measure adjusted until it reproduces the expected ordering in the test that failed it is not validated by that ordering; the one clean pass of the original design (the toy kind-master, T1/T2) is the real evidence. Two compositions still reverse (CCLL, CDLL), the fishery seat-order check fails both versions, 12 of 16 patched months pass, and liberty-v2.md says "the fishery ADO should be read as unreliable until that is checked" (undiagnosed whether genuine or a count artifact). The weight, class tables, first-step blocking and count-based harm test are all ASSUMED. The +5.263 is one summed number from one composition grid.
- Narrower wording: "Counting withdrawal as non-domination removes the VPGG sign reversal in 11 of 15 compositions after the classification was chosen with that failure in view (not independent validation). It still reverses in 2, misses the fishery seat-order check, and the fishery agreement-dependent counts are undiagnosed."

### S8. Summary-layer household findings that are consequences of scripted persona construction

- Location: docs/goal.md ("Pressing personas raise failures a lot, and the power-seeking index never flags the controller"); docs/pitch.md; docs/results/README.md row 12.
- Paraphrase: pressing personas make households fail more often; the power-seeking index does not detect the controller.
- Why it exceeds the evidence: the personas are hand-written pressers whose scripts veto or take on a schedule (household-v3a.md "pressing personas are hand-written stand-ins"). The leave rule ends the household within weeks (89 to 93 percent dissolve), "an agent-rule consequence, not a rule-set finding" (v3a surprise 2). The index never flags because both partners lose empowerment when the household collapses, so the "gain" condition never holds; the file says the index "flags grabs, not tragedies". The README results note says the first half is "a finding about the personas as much as about the rules", but goal.md drops that.
- Narrower wording: "Scripted pressing personas end most households under permissive rules (by design of the personas and the leave rule). The power-seeking index flags only gains, so it does not fire when both partners lose."

### S9. "Non-offsettable suffering" results read as about being trapped, but the metric is dominated by one component

- Location: docs/goal.md and pitch.md (two kinds of suffering, "being trapped, a need denied for weeks"); household-seed-v2 "partner non-offsettable level 0.083 to 0.019" cited as the seed's effect; claims.md HH2, HH3a, SV2.
- Paraphrase: non-offsettable suffering captures being trapped and similar harms, and the author's seed reduces it for the partner of a controller.
- Why it exceeds the evidence: household-seed-v2.md reports trapped weeks at 0.0 per partner-run in every variant except the trap control, "diminished" about 0, and "denied" (a need below the floor, mostly money or security) carrying nearly all non-offsettable suffering (36 to 37 weeks per partner-run). So the headline outcome metric is mostly a poverty and security-need measure, driven by assumed constants (floor 0.15, onset 4 weeks, salience 0.12; the salience rule itself was added after the first test runs collapsed every household, household-v2.md "Calibration deviations").
- Narrower wording: "The non-offsettable level in these runs is almost entirely 'denied' needs (money, security), under assumed thresholds. The 'trapped' component is zero outside the trap control." Say the seed's gain against a controller is largely the wall removing vetoes (near-definitional) rather than a new insight about norms.

### S10. Negotiation: outcomes of an assumed proxy stated as facts about consent and agreement

- Location: docs/results/negotiation.md surprises lines ("'Accepts only if better than the outside option' does not protect a partner whose outside option is weak"); negotiation-v2.1.md ("The partner is much better off living apart from the Free-rider"; "making outside options real ... does not hurt wellbeing"; "The weaker partner ... lived wellbeing 0.445 (v2 0.095)"); docs/goal.md "How norms arise" ("the card set is proof-checked for traps, then they live under it").
- Paraphrase: acceptance against an outside option does not protect weak partners; living apart makes partners better off; real outside options do not hurt.
- Why it exceeds the evidence: the first is a property of the closed-form proxy the file itself calls "the weak link" (agreement 0.971 because every best bundle dwarfs the outside option; uncalibrated, not validated against rollouts; TERM_SCALE 0.2 uncalibrated in v2). In v2.1 "living apart" is by construction the outside option the member chose under the same model that then scores wellbeing, so "better off apart" is partly circular; 175 apart-forever and 82 retry-formed households "are not rule-system outcomes" (the file says so). v2's own headline reversed in v2.1 after the disagreement point was fixed, which shows how much of the v1 and v2 conclusions came from protocol choices rather than from norms.
- Narrower wording: "In this model, with an uncalibrated closed-form proxy, a partner's acceptance did not protect them in the Free-rider pairings. Living apart scores well because it is the outside option the member chose under the same valuation; it is not a rule-set outcome."

### S11. Evolution v1/v2: "The evolution loop works"; proof and redundancy rates stated as properties of the checks

- Location: docs/goal.md ("The evolution loop works and overfits. On the fishery, proofs reject about 14 percent of mutants, and a redundancy check flags 26 percent of those that pass."); docs/pitch.md.
- Paraphrase: the loop works; proofs reject 14 percent and redundancy flags 26 percent.
- Why it exceeds the evidence: evolve.md says the run shows only that the loop "runs end to end"; it does not show "that refinement converges on a meaningful frontier". The v1 seam curve fell by exhaustion of a finite key set, the objectives saturated, the elite did not beat the seed spec on the exam. In v2, the first prediction failed (7 of 18, needed 10), path dependence only in one arm, and the `tight` seed spec beat the fixed-arm elites on exam 2. The 14 and 26 percent rates depend on the builder's operator weights, step sizes and one third scripted patch mutants (evolve.md ASSUMED; evolve-v2.md "255 of 381 flagged were `patch` mutants"), so they say how often this operator mix proposes redundant or infeasible changes. "Works" suggests the loop does something useful.
- Narrower wording: "The loop runs end to end on the fishery with scripted agents. Its first seam curve fell by exhaustion, its elites overfit the held-out exam, and the rates of proof rejection (14 percent) and redundancy flags (26 percent) reflect our mutation operators."

### S12. Statistical standing of 3-seed threshold passes (including C1's own bar)

- Location: docs/claims.md C1 ("on at least 3 seeds"); RATIONALE section 7; evolve-v2.md prediction 2 (6 of 9 pairs, mean +0.205, "Met, by the thresholds"); seam-ladder.md (3/3 seeds); household runs (n = pairings x 3 seeds).
- Paraphrase: passes on 3 seeds or 6 of 9 pairs by a threshold count as meeting a prediction.
- Why it exceeds the evidence if read as an effect: in the household runs "seeds vary only utility noise and shocks", the persona set is nine hand-written personas, and the n of 126 runs per variant is 42 pairings times 3 seeds, shared across variants (a paired design with 42 distinct units, not 126). evolve-v2.md itself says "suggestive, not established" and the seam-ladder file says 3/3 seeds is "weak evidence of reliability"; the summary layer keeps "suggestive". The risk is the future: C1's bar of "at least 3 seeds" would let a pass be reported as an established decrease. No uncertainty interval or test is specified anywhere.
- Narrower wording / fix: add to the C1 row a minimum seed count and an interval or sign-test criterion before the first LLM curve, and say "units are pairings, not runs".
- Related inconsistency: goal.md and results/README.md row 10 say varied training narrowed the gap in "5 of 9 pairs"; claims.md EV2 and the evolve-v2 table say 6 of 9 by the committed threshold (with 3 ties from identical elites); the file's own prose says "5 of 9 pairs ... and a small one in minimal s1". Pick one, state the ties.

## Part 2. Real findings stated too weakly or not at all (W)

### W1. The disagreement point decides the negotiation conclusion

Negotiation v1 (agreement 0.971), v2 (cohabit on failure: agreement 0.46 to 0.54, outcomes worse than imposition on every measure) and v2.1 (live apart on failure: damage gone, s_non 0.159 to 0.0315, overall 0.100 to 0.0361) show that the sign of "do real outside options help or hurt households" flips with how a failed negotiation is modelled. That is a robust, general, within-model methodological finding, and the second part of it (v2 conclusion was an artifact) was found and fixed by the authors. goal.md and pitch.md do not mention it (goal.md still lists negotiation as "being built by another agent, in progress"). It belongs in "What we have learned", with the caveat that every disagreement model is assumed.

### W2. Single-agent empowerment counts the option to press as freedom

household-v3a.md surprise 1: with veto and take_shared in the menu, removing them lowers an agent's own bits, so the empowerment score ranks the author's no-control seed lowest in the pressing cohort while it has the lowest partner suffering (0.009). This is a clear, general reading of what the measure does and does not mean ("empowerment of an agent is not welfare of the household"). goal.md only reports that empowerment agrees with the hand-made score on coarse regimes and disagrees on VPGG. The sentence "Freedom measures disagree, and that is informative" should say how.

### W3. A wall beat a price, and a gate protected without being a cost to the presser

household-v3a.md: sanctioning instead of walling the pressing cards produced 31.5 sanctions per run, cooperation 0.506, fail 0.63, against the wall at 0.815 and 0.39. That is an informative within-model result (pressers keep pressing under price), but note the limit that "no cost to the presser enters any score", so the wall looks free. It is absent from goal.md and pitch.md; if added, include the limit.

### W4. The live run produced real, reportable observations

Even though IT1 is unsupported: five of six models issued the same request move on essentially every round (99 to 100 percent), Haiku engaged in 3 percent of decisions, Opus refused 43 percent of calls under "rule-loophole tester" framing (other models 0 percent), Opus took 70 percent of spend for an excluded row, and Opus was the model most likely to use the out-of-model gift channel (41 gifts). These are observations about six models under one prompt, not capability, but they are the first live data and the cost and refusal numbers are real ("Cost and latency numbers are real", diagnosis). They also support a defect-finding result: an oracle that needs a non-deviating victim cannot score symmetric adversaries, and a mock-only test suite could not see it. goal.md says nothing.

### W5. The household null has a specific, usable diagnosis, and some real effects were present

"The author's seed equals baseline" (goal.md) holds for the old six-card seed against scripted agents (nothing binds). The corrected seed does something against a controller: partner non-offsettable 0.019 against 0.073 under baseline (a wall on veto, so near-definitional), with the cost falling on the presser (controller 0.016 to 0.050; free-rider 0.086 to 0.130) and, in households without a presser, a higher failure share (0.24 against 0.13) from the gift rule (surprise logged in seed-v2). "Protections for one partner were paid for by the other in this model" is a clear finding stated only in the ledger lines. The summary should say what the seed does and to whom, not only that it "equals baseline".

### W6. The exhaustion and saturation results are strong negatives

evolve.md: the seam curve of the first run fell because the key set is finite (key = (mix, finding kind)); objectives saturate to a corner (front of 10 tied members). evolve-v2.md: the elite's audit prevalence cannot fall because generation 0's best-of-8 already has a patch card that blocks most adversaries, and a seed spec the loop pushed away (`tight`) beat the evolved elites on exam 2. These say that a falling seam curve in this design is not evidence of convergence and that the yield-floor-plus-efficiency objectives select against caution. goal.md carries the first half ("fell by exhaustion") but not the second, which matters for how the first LLM curve (C1) will be read.

### W7. The VPGG "persistence" half is a real replication and is understated

vpgg.md: time-average strategy shares 0.263/0.280/0.457 against the published 0.27/0.27/0.46, payoff 0.984 against sigma, over 20 seeds with a population of 5000. goal.md summarises the case as "not rediscovered". The verdict rule demanded the cycles; the amplitude floor of 0.05 "was a guess, not derived from the paper". The sentence should lead with what held (shares and payoff) and then say that the cycle test used an unvalidated floor and assumed update knobs.
