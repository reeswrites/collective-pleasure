## F6. Model-world limits that now cap what any result can mean

These are not errors in any single file. They bound the claims the whole project can make.

1. **The agents only press, comply, stay or leave, with fixed weights.** The partner is scripted: the breach rule is a fixed suffering load (0.2), the leave rule ends households within weeks, personas never learn, deceive, coalesce or bargain over what they want. The S1 null, the 89 to 93 percent dissolution under pressing personas, and the H32 result that exit-driven convergence disappears at 5 percent exit noise are all consequences. Every result of the form "rules protect X" is conditional on a responder who cannot respond in the ways rules are meant to handle. Cap: household results cannot support any statement about LLM delegates (MU1 is not built) or about whether a norm survives a partner who adapts.
2. **The formal model reaches every harm the world permits.** This is the IT1 finding in general form. If the oracle's harm kinds are all reachable by the model checker, no agent can be "inventive" against them. The evaluator needs a world where the formal model is deliberately narrower than the action space, otherwise IT1 and the evaluator's reason to exist cannot be shown. The fix on the table (docs/plans/inventive-seam-next.md) does this for the ladder only; the household has the same property.
3. **Assumed parameters decide many conclusions (F5).** About a third of the multiverse rows flip. The relational share of pleasure comes from persona weights and the value of time together; "non-offsettable suffering" is almost entirely "denied" money and security under assumed thresholds; trust never moved.
4. **Household fidelity gaps (F4)** mean the model has no crowding-out channel, defaults act only as threat points, outside options change formation more than terms, and levers-off pleasure overstates repetition. A model with no crowding-out cannot evaluate rules whose risk is crowding out (claim F1, the central "rules versus training" contrast).
5. **The disagreement point decides negotiation conclusions** (v2 versus v2.1 flipped the sign) and the apart households are living arrangements, not rule-set outcomes.
6. **Single author, single day, and one agent family.** The loop (b) data, the four corrections, the answers to the read-backs and the choice of every assumed parameter come from one person. The prereg, the model, the run and the review are written by instances of the same model family. Liking versus wanting reports 15 held and 0 missed on exact solves of the author's own equations; predictions about a model's equations that the model's builder wrote are checks of the code, not tests of the idea. The pilot is disclosed, but a 15 of 15 should be read as an implementation check.
7. **Everything is one world with 3 seats.** The household has a fixed three-member cast. No result tests scale or a network.

- Recommendation. Add a page, "Limits of the model world" (one screen), linked from the one-pager and results README, listing these seven with the claims each blocks. Write the model-limit sentence into each prereg template: "this result can be about: ...; it cannot be about: ...". And for item 1, build the breach-cost agent (H05) and one adaptive-partner variant before any further rule-set comparison is cited as evidence about norms.
