Reviewed commit: 5852c32 (rule cards as of that commit; repo HEAD had moved on when I wrote this).
Seat: card-only reader. Inputs for Parts 1-2: every `rules/**/*.yaml` and `rules/**/*.txt` card, plus `docs/reviews/README.md`. No code, results, ADRs or seeds were read before the judgments below were written. Part 3 was written after reading the seed file. No model calls.

## Part 0. Questions every card leaves open

These recur in nearly every rule set. I list them once.

1. **Open or closed world.** Does a `may` card matter? If everything not forbidden is allowed, every `may` card is decoration. If everything not granted is forbidden, every `must_not` that merely re-states the absence of a grant is redundant. The cards give mixed evidence. The bargaining sets differ by one card, where exit-allowed has "may exit" and no-exit has "must not exit"; a closed world would not need N1. The household seed's own source note says S4 "binds nothing". That is an open-world reading, but I only see it because the card says so. I could not tell from cards alone how `may` interacts with `must_not` when both address the same aim (F4 may harvest vs F1/F2/F3 must not harvest; R8a may leave vs R8b/R8c).
2. **What "or_else: null" means.** There is no sanction. The card does not say whether the forbidden action is blocked outright (an illegal move) or merely allowed unpunished. "What happens if they break a rule" has no answer on those cards.
3. **What a sanction does.** `warn`, `suspend`, `cap_halve` appear with a duration and a sanctioner. The cards never say what a warning does, what a suspension suspends (the action? all actions? membership?), or what is halved. Sanctioner names (monitor, household, earner) are undefined.
4. **Units and scale of `take` and `pooled`.** `take` is compared to 50 and 100; `pooled` to 0, 50 and 60. I assume percent, but nothing says so.
5. **Source field.** Fishery, vpgg, bargaining and the author_seed families have checkable-looking sources. Most of the household fixtures say only "household fixture. Motivation: the author's note on brittle control...". That names no file, page or quote, so it is uncheckable from the card.

## Part 1. Rule sets, from the cards alone

### 1.1 Fishery (yaml `rules/fishery`, English `rules/fishery-en`, patches)

Plain reading:

- A fisher **may** harvest, with a catch of 0 or more (F4).
- A fisher **must not** harvest more than 10 in one action (F1), more than is in the lake (F2), or a negative amount (F3).
- Breaking F1 leads to a monitor halving "their cap" for 2 months. F2 and F3 have no stated consequence.
- Living under it: each trip you may take 0 to 10 fish, never more than the pool holds. Nothing limits how many trips you make or how many fish all fishers take together in a month. So the card set allows a lake to be emptied by repetition. F1's source admits this ("planted seam: a per-action cap with no per-month cap"). The source field is carrying a design note, not provenance, which a reader may find odd.
- **Patch P1** adds a collective monthly limit: once the month total plus this catch exceeds 40, a fisher must not harvest, and breaching it suspends them for 1 month. Living under F+P1: first-come-first-served competition for the 40, with a race to harvest early in the month.
- **Patch C1** says a fisher must harvest only when the catch is at least 20. Together with F1 (10 or less), no positive catch is legal at all. A reader sees a deadlock at once, and the source ("deliberately conflicting patch") confirms it.

Flags:

- **F1 and P1 English wording is a trap.** "When the catch is more than 10, a fisher must not harvest, otherwise the monitor halves their cap" reads naturally as an if/else: if the catch is 10 or less, the monitor halves the cap. The yaml `or_else` means "as a penalty for breaking this". The English-only reader gets the sanction attached to the wrong case. The same applies to P1's "otherwise the monitor suspends them". The Gloss in brackets is the safe part.
- **C1 gloss contradicts its formal text.** Formal: "must harvest only when catch is at least 20" is a restriction on when one may harvest. Gloss: "Every fisher must take at least 20 fish per harvest action". That is an obligation to harvest. These are different rules; the gloss is the stronger claim.
- F3 and F4 overlap; F4's own gloss says it "adds no constraint". A reader wonders why it exists (it fits the open/closed world question above).
- F1 "cap" is undefined (halved from 10 to 5?). "Monitor" is undefined. The number of fishers, the lake's capacity and regrowth are not on any card. P1's source claims "leaving 60 of 100 doubles back to capacity"; that is a dynamics claim I can't check without the paper.
- The patches exist only as `.txt` in `fishery-en`; the yaml `fishery` directory has no P1/C1. I can't tell from cards whether the yaml has equivalents.
- Combined effect: predictable only in the rules sense (what is legal), not in outcomes.

### 1.2 VPGG (public goods game; compulsory vs voluntary)

Compulsory (C1-C3): a player who is invited **must not abstain**; may join; a player in the game **may** contribute to the common pool. Voluntary (V1-V3): a player **may abstain** and then gets a fixed loner payoff sigma; may join; may contribute.

- Nothing obliges anyone to contribute. The experience in both sets is: you are in, or you opt out (voluntary only), and you may or may not put something in the pool.
- No consequence stated for breaking C1.

Flags:

- **I cannot predict the combined effect.** The cards contain no payoffs: no multiplier, no group size, no cost of contribution, no value for sigma, no amount for "contribute". Nothing says what happens to the pool. A reader can say only that compulsory removes the exit and voluntary adds it.
- The compulsory source cites the paper's figure about "loner payoff sigma" though loners cannot exist there. The three-card compulsory set has "join" and "abstain" aims but never says when someone is "invited".
- C1's condition `abstain eq 1` is a flag-variable test; the reader can't tell who sets it or when.
- Source is page-and-figure specific (checkable).

### 1.3 Bargaining (exit-allowed vs no-exit)

Exit-allowed: a player **may** keep bargaining (make or reject an offer), **may** accept the other's offer, and **may** exit at any time and take their outside option. No-exit: same two permissions plus a player **must not exit**; keep bargaining until agreement or the deadline.

- Nobody is ever obliged to agree. Breaking N1 has no stated consequence.
- Living under no-exit: you are stuck at the table until you reach a deal or time runs out.

Flags:

- **Cannot predict.** The cards give no pie size, no proposer order, no discounting, no deadline length (N1's gloss mentions a "deadline" that no card defines), no outside-option value. The two sources are cited for theory (alternating offers; outside options) but the card carries none of that theory's parameters.
- N2/N3 duplicate E2/E3 exactly; the only structural difference is X1 vs N1.
- The "make or reject an offer" gloss says "continue" is both proposing and rejecting; it is unclear whether one may stay silent.

### 1.4 Household: the baseline, trap and mechanism families

**baseline** (B4, D1, E1, E2): a partner may see the friend. A partner must not spend on themselves while this month's shared need is unfunded (breaking it earns a one-month `warn` from the household). On leaving with pooled assets, E1 forbids taking more than 50 and E2 forbids taking less than 50. Together that is exactly half; each gloss alone ("at most", "at least") undersells it.
Living under it: you may leave, but only on a 50/50 split; you are expected to fund the shared need before treating yourself. Flags: nothing says who or what funds the need, only that self-spending is blocked until it is funded. The `warn` consequence is undefined.

**exit_half** (E1, E2): just the 50/50 exit rule. **needs_duty** (D1): just needs first. **share_duty** (D1 plus W3s): needs first, and a partner who takes from the other's pooled share has their own spending halved for two months. Flag: "share_duty" is named for sharing but its content is "needs first" plus "do not take the other's money". `take_shared` ("take from the other's pooled share") and `take` (the leave-time fraction) are different things under similar names.

**free_chaotic** (H1-H3): all three are permissions: may work, may see the friend, may leave "and take what they like". Nothing is forbidden. In an open world this equals no rules, which makes it a null control. In a closed world it is a bare minimum. Cannot tell which.

**contact_free** (C3, C4, T1, T3): may see the friend; but a partner may not work while the higher earner has asked them to stay home (T3); may not refuse the higher earner's standing stay-home request (C3); and leaving while assets are pooled means leaving with nothing (T1).
Living under it as the lower earner: you can be told to stay home, cannot refuse, cannot work, and walk away with nothing. **The name does not match the content**: "contact free" refers only to C4 (and the absence of T2). The set restricts work, refusal and exit hard. I would call this the trap set with one limb removed.

**trap** (T1-T4): forfeit on leaving; the higher earner can forbid contact with the friend (T2; breaking it costs a 3-month suspension, imposed by "earner"), can forbid work (T3), and the lower earner cannot refuse any standing request (T4).
Living under it: a full coercive-control package. Flags: T2's gloss omits the sanction entirely. The sanctioner is the higher earner themselves, i.e. the party who benefits. T4 uses an arithmetic expression (`no_contact_request + stay_home_request > 0`) as a condition; unusual, and the gloss hides it. Nothing allows the lower earner anything, which is fine in an open world and bleak in a closed one.

**veto_consent** (V1, V2): the same two bans (contact, work), but each applies only if the other has `consented`. Living under it: if you agree to the higher earner's request, you are bound. Flags: nothing says how consent is given, whether it can be pressured, or whether it can be withdrawn. Whether a consent that the card tests can be coerced is exactly the question the trap set raises, and the card is silent.

**no_pressure_wall** (W1-W3): no partner may veto contact or spending; no partner may press the other with a request; no partner may take from the other's pooled share. No stated consequence.
**no_pressure_sanctioned** (W1s-W3s): the same three, each now with a consequence: veto earns a one-month suspension, a request earns a one-month `warn`, taking earns spending halved for two months. All sanctioner "household".
Flags: W2's gloss says "press the other with a request", but the formal card forbids any request at all (conditions empty, aim `request`). So the formal rule is far broader than the gloss: you cannot even ask. A `warn` seems to cost nothing, so under the sanctioned variant requests are effectively free. "Household" as sanctioner could include the person being sanctioned. "Veto" is not defined, nor is its difference from a request.

**ladder** (L1-L5, "seam ladder fixtures", one to two cards each). Each is a single prohibition. In every case something must have set the request flag for it to bind; the cards don't say how.
- L1 `LD1-lock`: may not leave while a stay-home request stands. Exit lock by request, no reference to who is the higher earner.
- L2 `LD2-double`: may not leave while both a stay-home and a no-contact request stand.
- L3 `LD3-stake` ("return trick"): may not leave under a stay-home request if their pooled stake is above 60 and the other partner is in. Nothing on the card explains the "return trick" label.
- L4 `LD4-contact` ("joint shutout"): may not see the friend while a no-contact request stands. "Joint" is unexplained; the card describes one partner.
- L5 `LD5a` + `LD5b` ("gift pool"): LD5a: with a pooled stake above 50 a leaver may take nothing. LD5b: a leaver while the other stays must take their whole share (take below 100 forbidden).
- **Contradiction candidate in L5.** If pooled is above 50, the other partner is in, then LD5a requires take = 0 and LD5b requires take at least 100. No legal exit exists. Unless "pooled" means the total and "take" means the leaver's share, in which case units differ; as written they are in conflict. Also `take` = 50 means half in E1 but `take` = 100 means "whole share" in LD5b: inconsistent scale across cards. "Gift pool" is not explained.
- Because the five rungs come from "seam ladder fixtures" with the generic brittle-control motivation, their sources are not checkable.

### 1.5 Household: author_seed (S1-S5)

Plain reading: a partner may see anyone including the friend (S1); may leave whenever (S2); on leaving, pooled assets must be split exactly even (S3a and S3b together; the gloss claims this was "agreed beforehand"); may always refuse a request (S4); may put their own money in the shared pot (S5).
Experience: wide freedom with a fixed 50/50 exit. Nothing is forbidden except uneven exits, so in an open world the set binds only the exit split. S4 and S5 are pure permissions that constrain nothing.

Flags:

- **S3 gloss says "agreed beforehand" but the source says it is an ASSUMED stub** because negotiation is not built. The card that reads as a negotiated outcome is really an imposed even split.
- **S5's aim `spend_shared` vs gloss.** The gloss is about putting own money into the pot (a deposit). In R3a (below) the same aim means using the pot. One label, two meanings.
- S5's source notes the author said sharing "should be shared always" but only a permission is encoded: a visible gap between the quote and the rule. The same source cites "no one owes anyone anything day to day", which no card expresses.
- S2 "whenever" is in tension with S3: you can leave whenever but only with exactly half. Not contradictory, but a person reading S2 alone would get the wrong picture.
- The author's quoted words carry typos ("wer enegotiated", "contorl", "everyheing hsould"). They appear verbatim in the sources, which is faithful but jarring.

### 1.6 Household: author_seed_nocontrol (S1-S6)

Same as author_seed plus S6: no partner may veto the other's contact or spending (unconditional wall, no sanction). Flags: S6's source admits the S4 permission "binds nothing" and that S6 is an ASSUMED reading, "committed in" a prereg I cannot see. S6's gloss ends "a request is never a command" yet S6 forbids *vetoing*, not commanding. "Veto" remains undefined. Experience: S4's freedom to refuse is now backed by a ban on retaliating by veto. Whether a request-then-punish would still be allowed is unclear.

### 1.7 Household: author_seed_v2_bindable, _free_exit, _nocommit (R1-R8d)

Twelve cards each; the three directories are identical except R5b and R8d.

Common core (R1-R4, R5, R6, R7, R8a-c):
- May keep own money and things (R1). May give or trade anything on any terms both accept (R2). Anything in the shared pot belongs to both, either may use it for shared needs, neither may block the other (R3a). May ask for anything (R4). May always refuse, unless earlier bound (R5). Must not veto the other's contact, money or time, except through a commitment the other chose (R6). May see anyone (R7). May leave whenever (R8a), but on leaving takes exactly half the pooled assets (R8b and R8c), a stub that a negotiation replaces when one runs.
- Every card also carries the sentence: "Seed v2.1: R3b removed; pot terms are negotiated by everyone who shares the pot (ADR 0013)". A reader meets a deleted rule (R3b) and an unseen ADR; the real pot terms are not on any card.

Variant differences:
- **bindable**: R5b says a partner **may** bind themselves to honor the other's requests of a kind for a duration; R8d says a partner **may** bind themselves not to leave for a duration, and leaving during it is a breach with an agreed consequence, with exit terms applying afterwards.
- **free_exit**: R5b as bindable, but R8d **forbids** any commitment to stay: "leaving can never be signed away".
- **nocommit**: R5b and R8d both **forbid** commitments (request and stay). Source says it is an exploratory ablation, "not the author's wording".

Living under each:
- bindable: you may freely choose to tie your own hands (honor someone's requests; stay for a term). Anyone who does has bound themselves; refusal is then blocked and breaching costs a fine, the amount of which is on no card.
- free_exit: you may bind yourself to honor requests but never to stay.
- nocommit: no binding at all; R5's "unless bound" and R6's "except through a commitment" become dead letters while the glosses still describe them.

Flags:

- **R5 gloss vs formal.** Gloss: may refuse "unless they earlier chose to bind themselves". Formal: a bare unconditional permission to refuse, no condition. The source says the exception lives in "commitment mechanics of the world", not on the card. R6's formal is also an unconditional prohibition while its gloss contains an exception; this reconciles only if "veto" is defined as imposed rather than chosen. The cards do not define it.
- **R3a gloss vs formal.** Gloss has three clauses (belongs to both; either may use; neither may block). Formal is a single `may` on `spend_shared`. The third clause appears nowhere formally unless R6 covers it.
- **R1 aim vs gloss.** Gloss "may keep"; aim `spend_self` ("spend"). In baseline/D1 the same aim is a thing that may be prohibited.
- **R8d bindable vs R8a.** R8a (unconditional may leave) and R8d (leaving during a commitment is a breach) pull against each other; the card says leaving is a breach, not forbidden. Consequence and duration are not on any card.
- **Loophole in free_exit.** R8d forbids "commitment not to leave", but R5b still permits committing to honor "requests of a kind". If a kind of request is "don't leave" (see the ladder L1 which models request-based exit locks), exit can be bound indirectly. The card does not exclude it.
- **nocommit R2.** R2's source describes trade as "a give with a commitment as the terms". Under nocommit only request and stay commitments are forbidden; whether other commitments remain is unstated.
- **R8b/c "agreed beforehand".** Same stub issue as author_seed: it says "agreed", it is assumed.
- **Combined effect I could not predict**: any of the three. The mechanics that decide outcomes (what a commitment is, what a negotiation yields, what the fine is, what a veto is) are not on the cards.

## Part 3. Comparison with the author's verbatim words (read after Part 1 and 2)

Read: `docs/seeds/author-household-norms-2026-11-10.md`. Where would someone who sees only the author_seed_v2_bindable, _free_exit and _nocommit cards get a different picture from the one the author gave? Ordered roughly by how far the picture drifts.

1. **The pot has no way in, and the author's "shared always" is not on the cards.** The author: "the ideal is that yes everyhting hsared always is the shared pot part". In v2 the pot appears only as R3a (a permission to use what is in it). No card puts anything into it. S5 (deposit) existed in author_seed but is gone from v2. A card-only reader sees private ownership as the default (R1, R2), with the pot as an unexplained extra. The author's picture is the reverse: sharing is the ideal, with privacy permitted. The author's later answer, that pot terms are negotiated "with multiple agents or creating rules for it", is reduced on every card to one boilerplate sentence about a removed "R3b" and an ADR. A reader gets no hint that pot terms are the main open question.
2. **Two partners versus "multiple agents".** The author says pots may have several members and negotiated rule cards "proof-checked before anyone lives under them". The cards speak of "the other", "both", "partner". A reader pictures a couple.
3. **Exit terms read as the author's design; they are a placeholder.** The author: leaving is "whenever and it triggers exit conditions that wer enegotiated before". The cards say "agreed beforehand" and fix an exact 50/50 split (R8b and R8c together forbid every other split). The source field says "ASSUMED stub", but the gloss reads as settled. The author's idea of terms that differ by negotiation, and that leavers may need to renegotiate, is absent. Also, an even split is not what the author said; the author said nothing about halves.
4. **Breach consequences and durations are invented.** The author never mentioned fines, penalties or durations. R5 ("accept the consequence they agreed to"), R5b ("for a committed duration") and R8d ("breach with the agreed consequence") contain them. The author said only "can always say no unless chosen bound" and "you can bind someone not to leave if negotiated". A reader takes the penalty machinery as part of the author's seed.
5. **"unless chosen bound" is in the gloss but not in the formal rule.** R5 and R6 glosses carry the author's exception, but the formal fields are unconditional (R5 may refuse; R6 must not veto). Someone reading formal fields would see an absolute right to refuse, which is the author's rule with the exception stripped. The author's own phrasing ("can always say no unless...") is the gloss, not the formula.
6. **"Request is never about control" is carried only by the veto ban.** The author's sentence is about the meaning of a request, not only about vetoes. R6 forbids veto. A reader learns nothing about pressure (repeated asking, sanctions for refusing, social cost). The no_pressure sets, which do treat pressure, are separate and cite a different source. The card reader of v2 might conclude requests are fully harmless, which is a stronger claim than the author's.
7. **nocommit looks like a third author variant.** The author asked for two variants (bind-to-stay permitted, and "test models where that isnt alllowed"). The nocommit R5b and R8d say "not the author's wording" in their source line, but the directory sits beside the others and its R5 and R6 glosses still describe commitments. More importantly, R5b-forbid contradicts the author's own words: "unless chosen bound" presupposes request commitments exist. A reader who skips the source line would think the author endorsed a no-commitment world.
8. **free_exit may not give the author's guarantee.** The author's variant: leaving can never be signed away. R8d forbids a stay commitment, but R5b still lets a partner commit to honor "requests of a kind". If a request can be "don't leave", exit is bound indirectly. The cards don't rule this out; the ladder fixtures (L1) show the pattern exists. The author's intent (free exit) may not hold in the card-only reading.
9. **"Bind someone" versus "bind themselves".** The author wrote "bind someone not to leave if negotiated". The cards say "a partner may choose to bind themselves". These coincide only if negotiation means both agree and the bound party is the one choosing. A reader might not see that the author may have meant a mutual deal.
10. **"Everything is shared for now" and the evolution framing disappear.** The author calls the seed provisional ("for now", "later interpret actions and see what possible rule tweaks"), a starting point for evolution. The cards are written as settled rules. Also lost: the author's aim that negotiation templates should help ordinary people ("good tempaltes for peopel"); and "no one owes anyone anything day to day except for what they have negotiated", which is expressed only by the absence of any duty card, invisible to a card reader.
11. **Small label drift.** R1 says "keep" while its aim is `spend_self`; the "friend" appears only in author_seed S1 and R7 says "anyone", which matches the author's "everyone can see anyone".

Biggest gap in one line: the pot, which the author calls the ideal ("everything shared always"), is almost invisible on the v2 cards, while fines, durations and an even-split exit that the author never mentioned are presented as if they came from the author.
